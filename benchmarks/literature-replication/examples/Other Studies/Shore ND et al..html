📋 Executive Summary

Original Study: The PRONOUNCE trial was a randomized Phase 3b study comparing cardiovascular outcomes between degarelix (GnRH antagonist) and leuprolide (GnRH agonist) in men with prostate cancer and pre-existing cardiovascular disease. The trial enrolled 2,226 propensity-matched patients (1,113 per arm) and found no significant difference in MACE (HR 1.18, 95% CI 0.86-1.61, p=0.28), but noted higher all-cause mortality with degarelix (HR 1.48, 95% CI 1.01-2.18, p=0.045).

Replication Result: Directionally Divergent

The UCSF replication found a statistically significant higher MACE risk with degarelix (HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.04-1.79, p=0.026), whereas the original trial found no significant difference. This divergence may reflect selection bias, unmeasured confounders in observational data, or population differences at an academic medical center.

⚠️ Limitations Analysis

Data Availability Limitations

Single-Center Cohort: UCSF represents a tertiary academic medical center, which may have different patient acuity, socioeconomic mix, and treatment patterns compared to the multicenter PRONOUNCE trial population.
Sample Size: The UCSF cohort (3,275 patients) is 147% of the original trial size, reducing statistical power for subgroup analyses.
Unbalanced Arms: UCSF has 704 degarelix vs 2,571 leuprolide patients (0.27:1 ratio), unlike the 1:1 randomization in PRONOUNCE. No propensity score matching was applied.

Methodological Limitations

Observational Design: Unlike the randomized PRONOUNCE trial, this replication uses observational EHR data with potential for confounding by indication and selection bias.
Outcome Ascertainment: MACE events identified through ICD diagnosis codes may have different sensitivity/specificity than adjudicated clinical events in the original trial.
Crossover Not Assessed: The original trial had significant early degarelix discontinuation (median 48 days). Crossover patterns in UCSF data were not analyzed.

🔍 Interpretation & Conclusions

Key Finding: The UCSF replication shows a statistically significant increase in MACE risk with degarelix (HR 1.36, p=0.026), which contradicts the original PRONOUNCE finding of no significant difference (HR 1.18, p=0.28).

Possible explanations for divergence:

  1. Selection Bias: Patients receiving degarelix at UCSF may represent a higher-risk population selected for the newer, more expensive agent due to perceived cardiac concerns.
  2. Confounding by Indication: Without proper propensity score matching, baseline differences between treatment groups may drive the observed association.
  3. Population Differences: Academic medical center patients may have different comorbidity profiles and disease severity compared to trial enrollees.
  4. Outcome Misclassification: EHR-based MACE identification may capture different events than trial-adjudicated outcomes.

Clinical Implications: This replication does not support superiority of degarelix over leuprolide for cardiovascular outcomes in real-world practice. The divergent findings underscore the importance of randomized trial evidence for treatment decisions. Further investigation with proper propensity score matching and sensitivity analyses is warranted.
