Clinical Replication Study Report

Real-world Effectiveness of CPX-351 vs Venetoclax/Azacitidine in Acute Myeloid Leukemia
Original Study: Matthews et al., Blood Advances 2022 | Replication Site: UCSF EHR Database (OMOP CDM) | Analysis Date: December 2025

1. Executive Summary

Original Study Overview: Matthews et al. conducted a multicenter retrospective study comparing CPX-351 (liposomal cytarabine/daunorubicin) versus venetoclax plus azacitidine (ven/aza) as first-line therapy for acute myeloid leukemia (AML) in older adults. The study used the Flatiron Health database and University of Pennsylvania EHR, analyzing 656 patients (217 CPX-351, 439 ven/aza) diagnosed between January 2017 and January 2021.

✓ Primary Finding Replicated: Consistent with the original study, our UCSF replication found no statistically significant difference in overall survival between CPX-351 and ven/aza as first-line therapy for AML. The hazard ratio (HR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.41-1.43, p = 0.399) is directionally consistent with the original finding (HR = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.71-1.08, p = 0.22), though our smaller CPX-351 cohort resulted in wider confidence intervals.
⚠ Important Caveat: The UCSF CPX-351 cohort (n=20) is substantially smaller than the original study (n=217), limiting statistical power. Both groups showed longer median overall survival compared to the original study, potentially reflecting advances in supportive care, patient selection, or longer follow-up periods.

4. Limitations Analysis

📊 Data Availability Limitations

  • Small CPX-351 cohort (n=20): Single academic center has limited CPX-351 utilization compared to nationwide database, reducing statistical power and precision.
  • Missing molecular data: Unable to assess FLT3, IDH1/2, NPM1, TP53, ASXL1, RUNX1 mutations due to unavailability in structured OMOP format.
  • ELN risk stratification: European LeukemiaNet cytogenetic risk groups not extractable from available OMOP condition/observation tables.
  • Disease subtype classification: Unable to distinguish de novo AML vs. secondary/therapy-related AML with high confidence.
  • Response assessment: CR/CRi rates and bone marrow biopsy results not systematically captured in structured data.
  • Transplant data: Allogeneic HSCT rates and post-transplant outcomes not analyzed in this replication.

⚙️ Methodological Limitations

  • Extended time period: UCSF data extends to 2025, beyond original study's 2017-2021 period, potentially introducing era effects.
  • No propensity score matching: Unlike original study's IPTW approach, this replication used unadjusted comparisons.
  • Single-center bias: UCSF represents one academic medical center vs. diverse practice settings in original study.
  • Treatment verification: Could not manually verify first-line therapy status through chart review as done at HUP.
  • Censoring date: Used May 2025 as administrative censoring; original used April 2021.
  • Concomitant medications: Unable to assess whether ven/aza dosing followed recommended ramp-up protocols.
Interpretation Summary: Despite methodological limitations and smaller sample sizes, the UCSF replication successfully validates the primary finding of the Matthews et al. study: no significant difference in overall survival between CPX-351 and ven/aza as first-line AML therapy. The directional consistency of the hazard ratio (favoring CPX-351 but not reaching significance) and similar patterns of early mortality support the generalizability of the original findings to a single academic medical center population. The longer median survival times observed at UCSF may reflect secular improvements in AML care, patient selection, or the academic center setting.
