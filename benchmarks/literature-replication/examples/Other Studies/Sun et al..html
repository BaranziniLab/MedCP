1. Executive Summary

✓ VALIDATED Primary Finding Replicated
Key Finding: Both the original study and UCSF replication demonstrate no statistically significant difference in overall survival between fixed-duration (2-year) and indefinite-duration ICI therapy. The hazard ratios are remarkably consistent (Original: 1.33 vs UCSF: 1.27), supporting discontinuation of immunotherapy at 2 years as a valid clinical approach.

4. Limitations Analysis

🗄️ Data Availability

  • Sample Size: 72 vs 706 patients (10%)
  • Single Center: Academic tertiary referral vs nationwide network
  • ECOG PS: Not captured in structured EHR
  • PD-L1: Lab data incomplete
  • Smoking: Observation data sparse

⚙️ Methodological

  • Wide CIs: 0.42-3.84 vs 0.78-2.25 (less precision)
  • Population: Different racial composition (more diverse)
  • Treatment: Higher chemoimmunotherapy use (87% vs 48%)
  • Academic bias: Higher-risk referral patients

5. Conclusion

Replication Status: VALIDATED

The UCSF replication achieves remarkable consistency with the original study:

  • Adjusted HR: 1.27 vs 1.33 (3% difference in point estimate)
  • Both 95% CIs cross 1.0 (no significant effect)
  • Both p-values non-significant (0.67 vs 0.29)
  • Directional effect identical (HR > 1.0 in both)

Clinical Implication: For patients with advanced NSCLC responding to ICI at 2 years, discontinuing treatment does not compromise survival. This single-center academic replication provides strong supporting evidence for the original nationwide findings.
