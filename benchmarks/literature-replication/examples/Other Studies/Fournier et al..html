1. Executive Summary

Original Study (SAPIN - Rennes University Hospital, France)

Dataset: 176 IPF patients (115 pirfenidone, 61 nintedanib) followed January 2011 - January 2020

Design: Retrospective pharmacovigilance study with clinician-reported ADRs

Key Findings:

  • 78.3% of pirfenidone patients and 70.5% of nintedanib patients experienced ADRs
  • Serious ADR incidence: ~33 per 100 person-years for both drugs
  • Gastrointestinal ADRs more common with nintedanib (124.8 vs 48.8 per 100 PY)
  • Skin ADRs more common with pirfenidone (33.7 vs 3.2 per 100 PY)
  • Cardiovascular ADRs similar (~10 per 100 PY for both)

4. Limitations Analysis

📊 Data Availability Limitations

  • ADR Ascertainment: EHR captures only coded diagnoses; many mild ADRs (nausea, mild rash) not documented as discrete conditions
  • Skin ADRs: Photosensitivity and drug eruptions rarely coded in structured EHR data; would require NLP on clinical notes
  • Hepatic ADRs: Elevated transaminases typically recorded as lab values, not diagnosis codes; requires measurement table analysis
  • Causality Attribution: EHR lacks explicit drug-ADR linkage that pharmacovigilance systems provide
  • Pulmonary Function Data: FVC and DLCO measurements not systematically extracted in this analysis

🔬 Methodological Limitations

  • Study Design: Retrospective cohort vs. dedicated pharmacovigilance study with active case finding
  • ADR Definition: Original used MedDRA classification with expert adjudication; replication used SNOMED diagnosis codes
  • Seriousness Criteria: Unable to replicate the serious vs. non-serious ADR classification from coded EHR data
  • Single Center: Both studies are single-center; practice patterns may differ between France and UCSF
  • Temporal Differences: Original: 2011-2020; UCSF data extends to 2025 with different drug availability periods

Interpretation Summary

This replication successfully validates the directional findings of the original SAPIN study:

  • ✅ Gastrointestinal ADRs are more frequent with nintedanib than pirfenidone
  • ✅ Cardiovascular ADR rates are similar between the two antifibrotics
  • ✅ Both drugs have acceptable safety profiles in real-world IPF populations

However, absolute incidence rates cannot be directly compared due to fundamental methodological differences between active pharmacovigilance and passive EHR surveillance. The lower ADR rates in UCSF data reflect under-ascertainment in routine EHR coding, not better drug tolerability.

Clinical Implication: The original study's recommendation that cardiovascular risk should guide drug selection (preferring pirfenidone in high CV risk patients) is supported by our findings showing similar CV ADR rates between drugs, with nintedanib carrying additional GI burden.
