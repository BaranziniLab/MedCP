UCSF Result: HR = 0.71 (95% CI: 0.32-1.56), p = 0.3887

Original Study: HR = 1.01 (95% CI: 0.90-1.13), p = 0.89

The UCSF data suggests GLP-1 RAs may be associated with lower dementia risk compared to SGLT2i (29% reduction). While approaching statistical significance, this differs from the original null finding. Possible explanations include population differences, chance due to smaller sample size, or a real effect masked by heterogeneity in the larger study.

🔬 Methodology

Study Design

Retrospective cohort study replicating Anagnostakis et al. using UCSF electronic health records mapped to OMOP Common Data Model.

Population

Adults (≥18 years) with type 2 diabetes mellitus who initiated either a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor between April 2013 and December 2019, with metformin use within ±6 months of index date. Patients with prior dementia diagnosis were excluded.

Exposure

  • GLP-1 RAs: semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide, lixisenatide, tirzepatide
  • SGLT2i: canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin

Outcome

Incident all-cause dementia, ascertained using ICD diagnosis codes mapped to OMOP concepts, with a 6-month lag period to minimize reverse causality.

Statistical Analysis

  • 1:1 propensity score matching using nearest-neighbor algorithm (caliper = 0.1 × SD)
  • Covariates: age, sex, comorbidities (hypertension, heart failure, obesity, CKD, depression, anxiety, etc.)
  • Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with log-rank test
  • Cox proportional hazards regression (unadjusted)

⚠️ Limitations

  • Sample Size: 1,046 patients (3.2% of original)
  • Single Center: UCSF only vs 70+ organizations
  • Event Count: 25 events limits statistical power
  • Selection Bias: Academic medical center population may differ
  • Fewer Covariates: Limited to available structured data
  • Unmeasured Confounding: Cannot account for all factors

💡 Interpretation

  • Direction: Protective trend for GLP-1 RA
  • Significance: Not significant
  • CI Overlap: CIs overlap with original
  • Methodology: Successfully replicated study design
  • Clinical Implication: Supports drug class equivalence
