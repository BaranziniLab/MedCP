Interpretation caveat: this is an observational EHR comparison, not a randomized contrast. The ICS-inclusive group may be enriched for patients with more severe disease, asthma overlap, prior eosinophilia, or clinician-perceived steroid responsiveness.

Possible immunologic explanation:

- **LABA/LAMA benefit** may reflect improved bronchodilation, reduced hyperinflation, better mucus clearance, and fewer symptom-driven urgent visits without adding steroid-associated infection risk.
- **ICS benefit is phenotype-dependent**: inhaled corticosteroids are most likely to reduce exacerbations in **type-2/eosinophilic COPD**, where IL-5/eosinophil biology and corticosteroid-sensitive airway inflammation contribute.
- In many frequent-exacerbator COPD patients, exacerbations are driven by **neutrophilic inflammation**, macrophage activation, epithelial injury, bacterial colonization, IL-8/CXCL8, TNF, IL-1β, and inflammasome-related pathways—processes that are often less corticosteroid-responsive.
- ICS can also increase susceptibility to airway infection or pneumonia by dampening local innate immune responses, potentially offsetting benefit in non-eosinophilic or infection-prone COPD.

So, the data here favor **LABA/LAMA overall**, while the biologic rationale suggests **ICS-inclusive therapy may still be preferable in an eosinophilic/type-2 or asthma-overlap subgroup**.
