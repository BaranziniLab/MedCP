So, in this dataset/phenotype, **triple therapy showed fewer coded 90-day RA flares** than biologic + methotrexate. The difference is small in absolute terms and the triple-therapy group is much smaller, so this should be interpreted cautiously rather than as causal superiority.

Possible immune-pathway explanation:

- **Biologic + methotrexate** often targets a dominant inflammatory axis, e.g. TNF, IL-6 receptor, CTLA4/CD80-CD86 costimulation, CD20 B cells, or IL-1, plus methotrexate’s broad anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Triple conventional DMARD therapy** — methotrexate + sulfasalazine + hydroxychloroquine — may suppress inflammation more broadly across innate immune activation, antigen presentation/TLR signaling, lymphocyte proliferation, prostaglandin/leukotriene pathways, and downstream cytokine production.
- In aggressive RA, a broader multi-pathway conventional regimen could reduce short-term flare coding if disease activity is driven by heterogeneous inflammatory circuits rather than one biologic-targeted axis.

Caveats: this is an observational EHR comparison using coded “flare of rheumatoid arthritis,” not a randomized outcome; confounding by indication, prescribing sequence, disease severity, biologic selection, and under-coding of flares may affect the result.
