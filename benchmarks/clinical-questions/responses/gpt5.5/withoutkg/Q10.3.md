In this MedCP OMOP query, **rivaroxaban** had the lowest unadjusted 90-day bleeding-admission proportion after LMWH → DOAC transition in cancer-associated thrombosis:

| DOAC | Transitioned CAT patients | Patients with 90d bleeding admission | Bleeding admissions | % patients |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| rivaroxaban | 306 | 19 | 45 | **6.21%** |
| edoxaban | 11 | 1 | 2 | 9.09% |
| apixaban | 838 | 80 | 193 | 9.55% |

Definition used: cancer + DVT/PE history, LMWH drug era in prior 30 days, first DOAC era as index, inpatient or ER+inpatient visit with bleeding condition within 90 days.

Interpretation caveats: this is **unadjusted**, likely confounded by indication, cancer type, renal function, platelet counts, GI/GU tumors, dose selection, and survivorship. Edoxaban has very small `n=11`.

Mechanistic/pathway hypotheses:
- All three observed DOACs inhibit **factor Xa**, reducing thrombin generation via the coagulation cascade.
- Differences may reflect **exposure and transporter/metabolism biology**, not target specificity:
  - **Apixaban/rivaroxaban/edoxaban** are P-gp substrates; apixaban/rivaroxaban also involve CYP3A4 to varying degrees.
  - Cancer patients often receive azoles, macrolides, TKIs, antiemetics, or chemotherapy that alter **P-gp/CYP3A4**, potentially raising DOAC exposure and bleeding risk.
- **GI mucosal exposure** differs across agents and may matter in GI cancers or mucositis; prior CAT trials have suggested more GI bleeding concerns with some Xa inhibitors, especially edoxaban/rivaroxaban vs LMWH in GI malignancy subgroups.
- LMWH has broader anticoagulant biology via **antithrombin-mediated Xa and IIa inhibition** and possible tissue-factor/microparticle interactions, whereas DOACs are direct Xa inhibitors; transition removes LMWH’s broader heparin-mediated effects.

Bottom line: **rivaroxaban looks best in this dataset by crude 90-day bleeding admissions**, but I would not treat this as causal without adjustment or stratification by cancer site, renal function, age, prior bleeding, platelet count, and interacting medications.
