**Lower 60-day steroid-rescue proportion: TNF inhibitors** in this dataset.

Biologic rationale: in severe UC, rescue need within 60 days is driven by rapid inflammatory control. TNF blockade directly suppresses a central inflammatory effector axis — TNF-driven macrophage/T-cell activation, endothelial activation, cytokine amplification, epithelial barrier injury, and leukocyte recruitment — and tends to have faster onset in acute severe colitis. Vedolizumab blocks α4β7–MAdCAM-1 gut lymphocyte trafficking, which is gut-selective and safer systemically, but it mainly prevents new gut-homing lymphocyte entry and often has slower induction; it may not rapidly extinguish an already established severe inflammatory infiltrate. That biology supports why TNF inhibitors can show less early steroid rescue.

Caveat: this is an observational EHR estimate using diagnosis-code severity proxies and steroid exposure as a rescue proxy; confounding by indication, prior biologic exposure, inpatient steroid continuation, and treatment sequencing could affect the comparison.
