**Yes — in severe asthma, IL-5/IL-5R–targeting biologics would be expected to produce substantially greater 90-day blood eosinophil suppression than anti-IgE therapy.**

- **Anti–IL-5 ligand therapies**:  
  - **Mepolizumab** and **reslizumab** bind IL-5, reducing eosinophil maturation, survival, and recruitment.
  - They typically cause marked blood eosinophil reductions over weeks.

- **Anti–IL-5Rα therapy**:  
  - **Benralizumab** binds IL-5 receptor alpha on eosinophils and basophils.
  - Its Fc domain recruits NK-cell–mediated antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, causing rapid and often near-complete eosinophil depletion.

- **Anti-IgE therapy**:  
  - **Omalizumab** binds circulating IgE, reduces FcεRI expression on mast cells/basophils/dendritic cells, and dampens allergic inflammation.
  - It may indirectly reduce eosinophilic inflammation in allergic asthma, but it does **not directly block eosinophil survival signaling or deplete eosinophils**, so 90-day eosinophil suppression is usually smaller and more variable.

**Bottom line:** IL-5/IL-5R biologics directly target the eosinophil axis, so they generally achieve stronger 90-day eosinophil suppression than anti-IgE therapy, whose primary mechanism is upstream allergic IgE blockade.
