Biological links to airway disease likely include:

- **Systemic inflammation:** COPD, obesity, diabetes, and dyslipidemia share chronic low-grade inflammatory pathways involving IL-6, TNF-α, CRP, oxidative stress, and innate immune activation.
- **Insulin resistance and altered immunity:** Diabetes may impair macrophage/neutrophil function, increase infection risk, worsen exacerbations, and promote airway remodeling through advanced glycation end-products and endothelial dysfunction.
- **Adipose–lung axis:** Obesity can mechanically reduce lung volumes, increase dyspnea, and biologically amplify inflammation via leptin/adiponectin imbalance.
- **Lipid metabolism:** Hyperlipidemia may reflect shared smoking/age/cardiometabolic risk, but lipids also influence surfactant biology, macrophage function, and vascular inflammation.
- **Bone/mineral disease:** Osteoporosis and vitamin D deficiency may connect through corticosteroid exposure, inactivity, smoking, systemic inflammation, and possible immune effects of vitamin D.
- **Thyroid disease:** Hypothyroidism can worsen fatigue, respiratory muscle function, and ventilatory drive; hyperthyroidism can increase metabolic demand and dyspnea.
- **Gout/urate biology:** Hyperuricemia can mark oxidative stress, hypoxia, renal/cardiometabolic disease, and systemic inflammation.

Caveat: these are **coded EHR prevalences**, so they reflect diagnosis/documentation patterns as well as biology; they should not be interpreted as causal without age, sex, smoking, medication, and healthcare-utilization adjustment.
