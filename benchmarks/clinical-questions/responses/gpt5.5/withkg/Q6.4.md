Biology from the KG points to several plausible bridges between COPD and these comorbidities:

- **Shared inflammatory/immune genes**: COPD shares many disease-associated genes with type 2 diabetes, obesity, hypothyroidism, and osteoporosis. Recurrent examples include `CXCL8`, `IL6`, `TNF`, `IL1B`, `TGFB1`, `NFKB1`, `IL10`, `APOE`, and HLA genes.
- **Cytokine signaling**: shared genes were enriched in “regulation of cytokine production,” “inflammatory response,” “immune response,” and “cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction.”
- **Lipid/metabolic signaling**: `APOE`, `FTO`, `ADCY5`, `DGKB`, and lipid-response biological processes connect COPD with hyperlipidemia, obesity, and diabetes.
- **Airway remodeling pathways**: `TGFB1`, `SMAD3`, `BMP4/BMP6`, `MMP1`, `ELN`, and `RUNX2` link systemic metabolic/endocrine disease to epithelial proliferation, extracellular matrix remodeling, smooth-muscle proliferation, emphysema/remodeling biology, and osteoporosis.
- **Steroid/inflammation axis**: osteoporosis and vitamin D deficiency are common in COPD and plausibly reflect systemic inflammation, reduced activity, smoking burden, age, and corticosteroid exposure; the KG-supported `TNF/IL6/TGFB1` axis provides a mechanistic overlap.

Caveat: these are **EHR-coded prevalences**, not adjusted epidemiologic risks. Broad parent concepts such as “metabolic disease” and “endocrine disorder” overlap with child diagnoses, so the more clinically interpretable headline comorbidities are **hyperlipidemia, diabetes/T2D, obesity, hypothyroidism, osteoporosis, and vitamin D deficiency**.
