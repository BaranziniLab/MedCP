Triple conventional DMARDs had the lower observed 90-day flare proportion, but the difference was tiny and based on few events.

| Strategy | RA patients | 90-day flares | Flare proportion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Biologic + methotrexate | 733 | 8 | 1.09% |
| Triple conventional DMARDs: methotrexate + sulfasalazine + hydroxychloroquine | 111 | 1 | 0.90% |

Interpretation: in this OMOP query, triple conventional DMARDs were slightly lower: 0.90% vs 1.09%. I would not over-interpret this as superiority because the event counts were very small, the triple-DMARD cohort was much smaller, and this was an unadjusted observational comparison.

Mechanistic/pathway explanation from the KG:
- Biologic + methotrexate directly hits high-level immune nodes:
  - TNF inhibition: adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, golimumab, certolizumab → `TNF`
  - IL-6 signaling blockade: tocilizumab → `IL6R`
  - T-cell costimulation blockade: abatacept → `CD80/CD86`
  - B-cell depletion: rituximab → `MS4A1/CD20`
- Triple conventional DMARDs are broader and less target-specific:
  - methotrexate: folate/adenosine-linked effects; targets included `DHFR`, `ATIC`, and immune-associated pathways including T-cell receptor, TNF-alpha, IL-1/IL-11, and B-cell receptor signaling
  - hydroxychloroquine: innate immune/TLR modulation, including `TLR7` and `TLR9`
  - sulfasalazine: inflammatory mediator pathways, including `ALOX5`, `CASP1`, and related inflammatory/oxidative stress nodes

The KG also linked RA to cytokine-receptor signaling, T-cell activation, B-cell activation, TNF-alpha signaling, IL/JAK-STAT-related pathways, and inflammatory-response pathways. So biologics may suppress dominant cytokine or lymphocyte axes more specifically, while triple therapy may cover multiple upstream/downstream inflammatory mechanisms simultaneously; in this dataset, that broader coverage corresponded to a marginally lower 90-day flare proportion.
