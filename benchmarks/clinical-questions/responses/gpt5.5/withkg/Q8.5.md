| Treatment class | Drugs | n with baseline + 90d eos | Mean baseline eos/µL | Mean min 90d eos/µL | Mean % suppression |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **IL-5/IL-5R** | mepolizumab, reslizumab, benralizumab | 35 | 986 | 236 | **65.5%** |
| **Anti-IgE** | omalizumab | 24 | 289 | 250 | **~0% / no consistent suppression** |

Using average rather than minimum 90-day eosinophils gave a similar pattern: **~60.8% suppression** for IL-5/IL-5R vs no meaningful suppression for anti-IgE.

**Mechanistic difference**

- **Mepolizumab / reslizumab** bind **IL-5**, preventing IL-5 signaling that supports eosinophil maturation, survival, recruitment, and activation.
- **Benralizumab** binds **IL-5Rα / CD125** on eosinophils and basophils; because it is afucosylated, it strongly induces antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, often producing near-depletion of eosinophils.
- **Omalizumab** binds free **IgE**, reducing FcεRI-mediated allergic mast-cell/basophil activation. It treats allergic asthma upstream of immediate hypersensitivity but does **not directly block eosinophil survival signaling**, so eosinophil suppression is weaker and less predictable.

Caveat: this is an observational EHR estimate with small matched-lab counts and no confounding adjustment, so it supports the expected pharmacologic pattern rather than proving comparative efficacy.
