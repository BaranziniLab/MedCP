## Agent's Response

In this OMOP EHR query, the **highly beta-1 selective beta-blocker class** had the slightly lower 90-day all-cause readmission proportion:

| Beta-blocker class | N | 90-day readmissions | Proportion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Highly beta-1 selective | 377 | 203 | **0.5385** |
| Less selective | 221 | 121 | 0.5475 |

So the observed difference is small: **53.9% vs 54.8%**, favoring highly beta-1 selective agents.

Drug-level counts showed the result was driven mostly by **metoprolol** in the beta-1 selective group and **carvedilol** in the less-selective group:

- Metoprolol: 186/348 = 0.5345
- Carvedilol: 96/180 = 0.5333

Mechanistically, the KG supports the comparison through adrenergic receptor binding:

- **Metoprolol, bisoprolol, atenolol, nebivolol** map primarily to **ADRB1 / beta-1 adrenergic receptor antagonism**.
- **Carvedilol, propranolol, labetalol** bind more broadly, including **ADRB1**, **ADRB2**, and for carvedilol/labetalol also **alpha-adrenergic receptors**.
- ADRB1 is linked to cardiac signaling pathways including **“Major receptors targeted by epinephrine and norepinephrine,” “Calcium regulation in cardiac cells,”** and cardiomyocyte signaling.
- ADRB2 is also involved in GPCR/cardiac signaling, but the KG annotates ADRB2 with processes including **negative regulation of cardiac muscle cell apoptotic process** and **positive regulation of cardiac muscle cell contraction**, so broader beta-2 blockade could plausibly alter compensatory cardioprotective or vascular signaling.

Caveat: this is an unadjusted cohort proportion, not a causal estimate. The difference is minimal and may reflect confounding by indication, drug selection, illness severity, or discharge practice rather than a true class effect.
