## Agent's Response

No. In this OMOP query, initial carbapenem therapy was associated with *higher*, not lower, 60-day UTI recurrence than narrow-spectrum therapy.

| Initial therapy | N | 60-day recurrence | Risk |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Carbapenem only | 119 | 72 | 60.5% |
| Narrow-spectrum only | 467 | 176 | 37.7% |

Effect estimates:

- Risk difference: +22.8 percentage points for carbapenem
- Risk ratio: 1.61, 95% CI 1.33–1.93
- Odds ratio: 2.53, 95% CI 1.68–3.83

Cohort definition used:

- First observed gram-negative UTI condition per patient:
  - E. coli UTI
  - Klebsiella UTI
  - Pseudomonas UTI
  - Proteus UTI
  - coliform UTI
- Required observation through 60 days after index date.
- Initial therapy defined as drug exposure on index date or next day.
- Carbapenems: meropenem, ertapenem, imipenem, doripenem descendants.
- Narrow-spectrum agents: nitrofurantoin, TMP, SMX, cephalexin, fosfomycin, amoxicillin/clavulanate-related descendants.
- Recurrence: any later UTI condition >1 day and ≤60 days after index.

Important caveat: this is unadjusted and very likely confounded by indication; carbapenems are probably being used in sicker or more resistant infections. So the result should *not* be interpreted causally as carbapenems increasing recurrence. It simply does not support a recurrence-reduction benefit in this crude analysis.
