Clinically, the substance-use diagnoses most often seen with bipolar disorder are typically:

1. **Alcohol use disorder** — usually the most common comorbid SUD.
2. **Cannabis use disorder** — very common, especially in younger bipolar cohorts.
3. **Nicotine/tobacco dependence** — often highly prevalent, though sometimes coded separately from “SUD.”
4. **Stimulant/cocaine use disorder** — important because dopaminergic activation can worsen mania/psychosis.
5. **Opioid use disorder** — less consistently top-ranked than alcohol/cannabis/tobacco, but clinically significant.
6. **Sedative/anxiolytic use disorder** — often linked to insomnia/anxiety self-medication.

Plausible neurotransmitter links:

- **Dopamine mesolimbic reward system**: mania, impulsivity, reward salience, cocaine/stimulants, alcohol reinforcement.
- **GABA–glutamate balance**: alcohol, sedatives, withdrawal hyperexcitability; also relevant to mood stabilizers.
- **Endocannabinoid system**: cannabis, reward modulation, stress response, dopamine/glutamate interactions.
- **Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors**: tobacco dependence, dopamine release in reward circuits.
- **Opioid peptide / μ-opioid system**: opioid use, alcohol reward, hedonic tone.
- **Serotonin and norepinephrine systems**: mood regulation, impulsivity, stress reactivity.

Bottom line: **alcohol, cannabis, and nicotine/tobacco** are the expected leading co-occurring substance-use diagnoses in bipolar disorder, with shared biology centered on **dopaminergic reward circuitry**, modulated by **GABA/glutamate**, **endocannabinoid**, **opioid**, and **nicotinic cholinergic** systems.
