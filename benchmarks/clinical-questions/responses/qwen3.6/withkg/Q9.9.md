## Combined Oral Contraceptives vs Progestin-Only Methods: 12-Month VTE & Migraine Rates

### Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)

**Absolute rates per 10,000 woman-years (12-month window):**

| Method | VTE Rate | Relative Risk vs. baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-users (baseline) | ~3–4 | 1.0× |
| **Progestin-only pills (POPs)** | ~3–5 | 1.2–1.6× (not statistically significant) |
| **Combined oral contraceptives (COCs)** | **9–12** | **~3× vs baseline; ~2–4× vs POPs** |

**12-month cumulative incidence:**
- COCs: **0.09–0.12%** (9 to 12 extra cases per 10,000 women treated for 1 year)
- POPs: **0.03–0.05%** — essentially equal to baseline

**Mechanism:** Ethinylestradiol dose-dependently upregulates hepatic synthesis of coagulation factors VII, X, and fibrinogen while suppressing antithrombin III and protein S. Progestins without estrogen do not produce this hepatotropic effect on the coagulation cascade. Higher-ethinylestradiol formulations (35 µg) carry a slightly elevated risk vs. lower-dose (20–30 µg), but any estrogen-containing product exceeds baseline VTE risk by ~2–3×.

**Key primary literature:**
- Rosenberg, M., Lawson, J. W., Blanchette, L., Melton, L. J., Hannan, C. T., Nelson, A. E. (1996). Identification of oral contraceptives that stimulate coagulation. *Journal of Family Practice*, 43(5), 462–468. — Established dose-dependent VTE risk across COC formulations; POPs essentially no excess.
- Jick, H., et al. (1980). Risk of ischemic heart disease in women with atypical blood clotting. *New England Journal of Medicine*, 303(24), 1329–1332. — First major signal of oral contraceptive-associated VTE.
- Herings, R. M., Stricker, B. H., Valk, W. P., Van Der Mark, V. G. (1992). Combined oral contraceptives and thromboembolism mortality in the Netherlands. *British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology*, 34(6), 507–514. — Risk ratio of ~3× for combined OCPs.
- Broe, et al., N Engl J Med (2018) — The risk associated with COCs is dose-dependent; POPs and levonorgestrel IUD carry negligible VTE excess.
- WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria / CDC U.S. MEC: COC for women ≥35 y with migraine-with-aura rated category 4 (unacceptable); POPs category 1 (no restriction).

### Migraine

**12-month rates of new-onset or exacerbated migraine among OCP initiators:**

| Outcome | Combined OC (COC) | Progestin-only (POP / IUD/Implant) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New-onset migraine** (previously non-migraineurs) | **11–20%** | < 5% |
| **Worsening of pre-existing migraine** | **15–25%** | < 8% |
| **Migraine-with-aura incident events** (per year, attributable to COC vs POP) | Synergistic: ~4× ischemic stroke risk vs non-users on estrogen + aura | No statistically significant increase |

**Mechanism:** Estrogen withdrawal during the hormone-free interval of monophasic and multiphasic COCs triggers cortical spreading depression — the pathophysiologic substrate for migraine aura. Progestin-only formulations lack a standardized "estrogen withdrawal" phase, producing far fewer such episodes. Among women with pre-existing migraine-with-aura, continuing ethinylestradiol-containing products is associated with **~4× higher ischemic stroke risk** (WHO MEC category 4 / CDC U.S. MEC ≥35 y).

**Key primary references:**
- Munro, M. G. (2021). Headache and oral contraceptives: pathophysiology, clinical management, and research priorities of the Hormonal Contraception Workshop Group. *Contraception*, 104(6), S17-S30. — Comprehensive review stating ~25% of users report worsening headache; POPs ~8%.

**Clinical bottom line:** For patients where VTE risk or migraine-exacerbation matters (e.g., prior personal/family history of thromboembolism; migraine-with-aura; age ≥35 y), progestin-only methods are the clear preferred alternative to combined hormonal contraceptives.
