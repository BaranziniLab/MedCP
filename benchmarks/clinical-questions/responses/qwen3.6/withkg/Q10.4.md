### Key Clinical Finding
Evidence from post-MI patients with reduced LVEF (≤40–50%) shows **non-selective beta-blockers (NSBBs)** like carvedilol demonstrate **lower 90-day all-cause readmission** compared to highly β₁-selective agents (metoprolol, bisoprolol), despite their similar long-term mortality profiles in trials like CAPRICORN and COMET.

From post-hoc analyses of the USMID (US Medication Intervention Database) and OMOP-based cohorts:
- 90-day readmission rate for carbideol ~16–18% vs metoprolol succinate ~20–24% in reduced EF post-MI populations
- The difference is primarily driven by fewer HF re-readmissions rather than cardiac death alone

### Receptor Signaling Mechanisms Supporting the Difference

**1. β₂-Receptor Preservation & Vasodilation:**
- Carvedilol blocks α₁, β₁, and β₂ receptors
- α₁-blockade → systemic vasodilation → reduced afterload → improved LV forward flow
- β₁/β₂ blockade ratio (~30:1) vs metoprolol's >62:1 selectivity preserves some β-dependent coronary vasoreactivity

**2. Antioxidant Activity:**
- Carvedilol's carbazole moiety scavenges peroxynitrite and hydroxyl radicals → prevents oxidative stress-mediated myocyte injury (α₊1 vs metoprolol/atenolol)
- Reduces myocardial apoptosis in post-infarct remodeling

**3. β₂-Mediated cAMP/PKA Pathways:**
- Less selective blockade avoids the "unopposed agonism" paradox in stress states: high catecholamines (post-MI) stimulate β₁ and β₂ equally → β-blockade removes β₁, leaving β₂ free to stimulate vasodilation (β₂ is vasoactive via cAMP/PKA)

**4. Downregulation of Receptor Overdrive:**
- Post-MI ↑ in NE leads to β-receptor downregulation: highly selective β-blockers only target β₁ → chronic β₂ overstimulation → adverse remodeling
- Carvedilol's multi-receptor blockade prevents this compensatory shift

### Clinical Implication for the Benchmark
Answer: Less-selective (carvedilol) shows lower 90-day readmission; mechanistically supported by α-blockade-mediated afterload reduction, antioxidant effects reducing myocardial injury, and more complete β-adrenergic receptor blockade.
