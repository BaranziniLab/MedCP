## Neurotransmitter Systems Linking Bipolar Disorder and Substance Use

A couple of angles emerge from the graph:

**1. Dopaminergic reward circuitry (the most established link)**
- Bipolar disorder involves dysregulated mesolimbic dopamine (hyperdopaminergia during mania, hypodopaminergia during depression). This maps onto addiction's reward pathway — making bipolar patients more susceptible to drug reinforcement and self-medication of depressive episodes.

**2. Glutamatergic dysfunction**
- The graph shows **glutamate/GluN2B** prominently connected to manic states. Both mania and substance use (especially cocaine) increase extracellular glutamate in the nucleus accumbens, suggesting a shared excitatory drive that may reinforce the comorbidity.

**3. GABAergic deficits**
- The graph links **GABA receptor agonists** to both bipolar symptom modulation and alcohol dependence treatment. Chronic alcohol consumption downregulates GABA<sub>A</sub> receptors, which overlaps with the inhibitory tone abnormalities seen in bipolar disorder.

**4. Noradrenergic / adrenergic systems**
- Mania is associated with **↑ norepinephrine** in LC projections (the graph confirms this), while nicotine dependence directly stimulates nicotinic acetylcholine receptors on noradrenergic neurons. The overlapping locus coeruleus hyperactivity may explain why smokers are so overrepresented in BD clinics.

**5. Serotonergic regulation**
- Though less emphasized in the query results, 5-HT dysregulation underlies both mood instability and certain substance-use pathways (especially alcohol's disinhibition effects). This is why SSRIs are problematic as monotherapy in BD but relevant to some comorbidity profiles.
