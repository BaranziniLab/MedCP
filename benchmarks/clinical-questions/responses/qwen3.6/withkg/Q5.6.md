**Most commonly used antidepressant classes in adults ≥65:**

1. **SSRIs** — First-line for most elders due to favorable safety profile. Common agents: sertraline, escitalopram, citalopram (dose-limited by QT concerns at higher ages).

2. **SNRIs** — Second-line; useful when SSRIs fail or pain symptoms co-occur. Venlafaxine and duloxetine are typical choices. Duloxetine is often preferred neuropathic pain comorbidity.

3. **NAssA (Mirtazapine)** — Popular in the elderly for its sedative effect (helps insomnia), appetite stimulation, and low anticholinergic burden. Useful in underweight or insomniac patients.

4. **NDRI (Bupropion)** — Can be used when sexual side effects, weight gain, or fatigue are concerns. Avoid in seizure risk or eating disorders. Activating profile suits lethargic depression.

**Less commonly used due to higher anticholinergic/cognitive burden:**
- **TCAs** — Generally avoided (e.g., amitriptyline, nortriptyline can cause orthostatic hypotension, dry mouth, constipation, Falls risk) though **nortriptyline** is sometimes used at low dose for neuropathic pain when benefits outweigh risks.
- **MAOIs** — Rarely used due to dietary restrictions and drug-disease interactions; reserved for treatment-resistant depression.

**Key geriatric considerations:**
- Start low, go slow (Beers Criteria recommends halving typical doses)
- Avoid highly anticholinergic agents (citalopram max 20 mg/day >60 yrs)
- Monitor for hyponatremia (SIADH), falls, and drug interactions
