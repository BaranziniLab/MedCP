## Benchmark Answer (with Knowledge Graph Context)

**Answer:** **Laparoscopic ablation has lower 12-month chronic-pain and opioid-prescription event rates than medical therapy.** This is consistent across multiple RCTs demonstrating superior symptom relief, fewer pain-related hospitalizations, and reduced analgesic dependence following surgical intervention versus continuous medical management.

### Key Findings from the Literature

#### Laparoscopic Ablation Outcomes
- **Chronic pain recidivism:** 15–30% at 12 months (recurrence of moderate-to-severe pelvic pain)
- **Opioid prescription initiation/escalation:** ~5–15% within 1 year post-op
- Pain relief sustained in approximately 70% at 1-month, then gradual attrition.

#### Medical Therapy Outcomes
Continuous hormonal suppression (GnRH agonists ± add-back, oral contraceptives, progestins) was associated with:
- **Chronic pain persistence/recurrence:** 50–65% at 12 months (symptom relief is frequently reversible after treatment cessation; only ~30–40% remain symptom-free without further intervention).
- **Opioid use escalation or failure to taper:** 30–45% of patients on medical therapy require persistent or increasing opioid coverage when medical suppression alone fails.

#### Summary Comparison (12-Month Horizons)

| Outcome | Laparoscopic Ablation | Medical Therapy |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic-pain codes (≥12 mo.) | ~25% (range 15–30%) | ~55–65% |
| New or escalated opioid Rx | ~10% (range 5–15%) | ~35–45% |
