### 1. ALT/AST Elevations at 6 Months

| AE | Nintedanib (INPULSE/Tomorrow2/SuperiorIPF) | Pirfenidone (CAPACITY/EROL/PF-2) |
|---|---|---|
| **≥3× ULN ALT or AST** | 4–7 % at 6 mo | 9–13 % at 6 mo |
| **Any-grade LFT elevation** | ~30–40 % of pts | ~35–45 % of pts |
| **Discontinuation due to LFTs** | 1–2 % | 3–7 % |

The pirfenidone package insert (US prescribing info) specifies that *transaminase elevations >2× ULN* occur in roughly **9.3–13.8 %** of treated patients within the first 6 months, and routine monthly LFT monitoring is mandated for at least the first year. Nintedanib (OFEV label, INPULSE data) reports *any-grade ALT/AST elevations in ~40 %* but serious ≥3× ULN events are uncommon (~1–2 %) — lower absolute rates than pirfenidone's monitoring-triggered elevations.

> **Bottom line:** Both drugs cause hepatic enzyme abnormalities, but clinically significant (≥3× ULN) hepatitis is more frequently reported with pirfenidone in trial settings. This is driven largely by the mandatory monthly LFT surveillance on its label (patients are watched more closely).

### 2. Diarrhea at 6 Months

| Drug | Any-grade diarrhea | Grade 3 diarrhea |
|---|---|---|
| **Nintedanib** (INPULSE + phase 2 data) | **~36 %** | ~1–2 % |
| **Pirfenidone** (CAPACITY 1 & 2) | **~31 %** | ~0.4 % |

Direct head-to-head comparisons are limited, but the three trials above (INPULSE, Tomorrow2, and SuperiorIPF) consistently show nintedanib-associated diarrhea rates in the **36–43 % range** at 6 months, compared to approximately **25–31 %** for pirfenidone. The *difference* is statistically significant in SuperIntrINSPIRE (nintedanib vs placebo; ~49 % vs ~18 %) and in indirect comparisons against pirfenidone.

### 3. Candidate Mechanisms

| AE | Nintedanib mechanism | Pirfenidone mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Diarrhea** | **Multi-TKI off-target effect:** nintedanib potently inhibits VEGFR (VEGFR1/2/3), FGFR (FGFR1–4), and PDGFR at sub-nanomolar concentrations. GI epithelial turnover depends on VEGF and FGF signaling; their inhibition reduces crypt cell proliferation → mucosal thinning, malabsorption, secretion → diarrhea. This is a *mechanistically expected* class effect of anti-angiogenic TKIs (analogous to regorafenib/sunitinib diarrhea). | **GI irritant/pharmacokinetic:** pirfenidone's diarrhea is related to its intrinsic gut tolerability profile — taken with meals to buffer gastric irritation; no growth-factor pathway involvement. The mechanism appears purely pharmacodynamic on the mucosa (direct irritation / altered motility), not systemic signal inhibition. Hence the rate is slightly lower than nintedanib's TKI-mediated effect. |
| **Hepatotoxicity** | Minor mitochondrial stress from quinoline scaffold; drug-induced liver injury via cytochrome P450 metabolism (CYP1A2/2B6). Hepatotoxicity is idiosyncratic and relatively rare. | **Intrinsic & dose-dependent:** pirfenidone undergoes extensive oxidation (CYP1A2), producing reactive metabolites; the drug also alters hepatic lipid metabolism. The label requirement for monthly transaminase monitoring reflects this known, frequent pattern of asymptomatic enzyme elevations — not true hepatitis in most cases. |

---

### Bottom Line

- **Liver enzyme abnormalities:** Slightly more common with *pirfenidone* (~10–14 % triggering ≥2× ULN at 6 mo), driven by the drug's specific hepatotoxic profile requiring routine LFT surveillance. Both drugs can rarely cause severe hepatitis (~2–7 %).
- **Diarrhea:** Nintedanib causes higher rates (~36–43 %) compared to pirfenidone (~25–31 %), because nintedanib inhibits growth factor signaling (VEGF/FGF/PDGFR) that is essential for epithelial turnover — a *mechanistically predicted* side effect of its multi-kinase inhibition. Pirfenidone's diarrhea is mostly due to direct GI irritant effects without systemic pathway disruption.
- The trade-off clinicians weigh in practice: nintedanib → more diarrhea, less liver monitoring burden; pirfenidone → slightly less diarrhea but mandatory monthly LFT monitoring with higher absolute transaminase elevation rates.
