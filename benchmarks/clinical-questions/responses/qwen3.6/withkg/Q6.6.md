## Most Frequent Extraintestinal Comorbidities in IBD

**Musculoskeletal (most common class):**
- Peripheral arthritis: 10–35% of patients — Type 1 (pauci-articular, parallels gut disease) vs Type 2 (polyarticular, independent course). Axial spondyloarthritis/ankylosing spondylitis affects ~5%.

**Gastrohepatic:**
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC): ~2–6% overall. Drastically enriched in UC (~30–70× population risk). PSC-IBD has a distinct phenotype and increased colorectal cancer risk.
- Gallstones & nephrolithiasis: Crohn's ileal involvement causes bile acid malabsorption → cholesterol gallstones; fat malabsorption → calcium oxalate kidney stones.

**Dermatologic:**
- Erythema nodosum (most common dermatologic, ~15% of IBD). Pyoderma gangrenosum (~1–5%).

**Ocular:**
- Anterior uveitis & episcleritis: ~3–20%. Parallel to gut flares — key clinical pearl.

**Oral:**
-aphthous stomatitis (canker sores) is very common (~10–30%).

**Others of importance:**
- Venous thromboembolism (DVT/PE): 3–4× population risk during active flares and post-op regardless. Hypercoagulability from systemic inflammation (↑ IL-6, TNF-α → ↑ factor VII and fibrinogen). 
- Osteoporosis: multifactorial — chronic steroid use + malabsorption + active inflammation itself (cytokines drive osteoclastogenesis).
- Sjögren's syndrome, Behçet's, and other autoimmune overlaps are rarer but recognized.

## Shared Immune Signaling Across IBD and Comorbidities

The MedCP knowledge graph returns ~100 Crohn's-associated genes, which cluster into a few functional modules that explain the multi-organ spread:

### 1. **IL-23 / IL-17 axis — the archetypal cross-tissue pathway**
- **IL18RAP / IL18R1 / TNFSF15 (TL1A ligand)** — all in the gene list. IL-23 drives pathogenic Th17 cells that produce IL-17, IL-22, and GM-CSF. Th17 cells infiltrate not just gut but synovium, skin, and eye in comorbid arthritis, erythema nodosum, and uveitis.
- **SMAD3** — regulates TGF-β–driven Treg development and balances against Th17; loss shifts toward inflammation at multiple sites.

### 2. **TNF / TNFR superfamily — systemic cytokine amplifier**
- **CD40, BTNL2, TNFSF15** — all in the gene list. Overproduction of TNF-α is central to Crohn's/UC mucosal inflammation AND drives joint (synovial TNF), skin, and hepatic (PSC-related biliary epithelial injury) pathology. Secukinumab and ustekinumab target TNF-α precisely because it acts as a systemic cytokine mediator.

### 3. **IL-2 / Treg signaling break**
- **IL2RA (CD25), PTPN2, BANK1** — IL-2 receptor signaling is required for regulatory T-cell (Treg) function. Loss-of-function in IL2RA or upstream phosphatase defects in PTPN2 impair central tolerance; this is shared with autoimmune diabetes, vitiligo, and psoriasis phenotypes. The immune surveillance break enables broader autoimmunity beyond the gut.

### 4. **Innate sensing & autophagy**
Genes in this set converge on innate barrier sensing—NOD2-like signaling (even if not named), MAP3K8 (TANK-binding kinase 1, a TBK1/IKK-ε family member upstream of NF-κB and IRF activation), CARD9 (fungal pattern recognition) — that link bacterial/fungal product detection at the intestinal epithelium to sustained systemic cytokine production.

### 5. **Adhesion & leukocyte trafficking**
Multiple integrin and adhesion pathway genes (ITGA4, ITGB3, SKAP2) enable immune cells to home to synovium, skin, and liver, completing the anatomical bridge from gut immune activation → distant comorbidity.

## Clinical Takeaway

The pattern is clear: **IBD is a systemic Th17/TNF-α–driven disease with a Treg-deficiency component**. The same cytokine networks (TNF-α, IL-23/IL-17, IL-6) that inflame the gut also drive joint synovium inflammation, skin lesions, hepatic biliary injury, uveal tract inflammation, and systemic hypercoagulability. This explains why biologics targeting TNF-α (infliximab), IL-12/23 (ustekinumab), IL-23p19 (risankizumab/guselkumab), or anti-integrin (vedolizumab, natalizumab) frequently improve extraintestinal disease—not just gut symptoms.
