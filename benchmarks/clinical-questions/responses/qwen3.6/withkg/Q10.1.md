### Key Clinical Takeaways

For Parkinson's patients **starting DAs**, the most notable early risks within 6 months are:

1. **Impulse control disorders **(ICDs)—pathological gambling, hypersexuality—are the hallmark adverse events of DA therapy. Levodopa does not cause ICDs at similar rates; this is specific to DA receptor stimulation (particularly D3-preferring agonists like pramipexole and ropinirole).

2. **Sudden-onset somnolence and sleep攻击**—patients can lose consciousness mid-conversation or while driving unexpectedly. Unlike mild daytime fatigue, these are "microsleep" events distinct from normal sedation.

3. **Orthostatic hypotension** is more pronounced with DAs vs levodopa due to DA-mediated vasodilation. The FDA black-box warning for rotigotine specifically notes this risk.

4. **Hallucinations and psychosis** occur in ~10-20% of patients on DAs, substantially higher than levodopa alone. Risk rises further with age (>65) compared by drug class.

5. **Dyskinesia** as an *initial* side effect is more common early on DAs (pramipexole/ropinirole have highest rates) — often dose-dependent and may require dose reduction or titration slowdown.
