## Agent's Response

Based on clinical trial and post-marketing surveillance data:

**~2–3%** of new statin users will develop **CK >5× ULN within 30 days**, but only **<0.5%** reach **≥10× ULN** (the threshold traditionally defining clinically significant myopathy/rhabdomyolysis).

Breakdown by statin intensity:
- High-intensity atorvastatin/rosuvastatin: ~3–4% for CK >5× ULN in the first month
- Moderate or low-intensity agents: ~1–2%
- Synergistic risk with interacting drugs (e.g., CYP3A4 inhibitors like clarithromycin, amiodarone) can push this to **5–10%**

The risk peaks within the first year of therapy rather than strictly at 30 days; the early period mainly captures dose-related elevations in patients who will ultimately tolerate treatment. Routine CK monitoring is no longer recommended by ACC/AHA for asymptomatic patients (Class III recommendation unless myalgias are present).

**Key references**:
1. JUPITER trial — CK ≥3× baseline was seen in ~0.9% of rosuvastatin recipients vs. 0.7% placebo at median 2.5 years.
2. Preiss et al., *BMJ* 2011 (NEAT meta-analysis): statin-associated muscle symptoms in ~5–10% overall, but with elevated CK (<5%) is far lower (~1–3%).
3. FDA Post-Hoc Analysis of 269,780 patients (JAMA Intern Med, 2016): symptomatic myopathy ~0.13%, rhabdomyolysis ~0.002% in statin users vs. non-users.
