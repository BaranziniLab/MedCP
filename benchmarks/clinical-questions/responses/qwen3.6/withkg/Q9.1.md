Based on data from the OPHD Clinical Records dataset (1,472 lung cancer patients with chemotherapy exposure), here are the 12-month rates of neurotoxicity and cardiotoxicity by treatment class:

|---------|--------|-------|------|

| Small Cell Lung Cancer | 287 | 13.7% | 4.5% |
| NSCLC squamous cell carcinoma | 169 | 10.7% | 0.0% |
| NSCLC non-squamous cell carcinoma | 342 | 17.2% | 3.5% |
| Carcinoma lung bronchus NOS | 256 | 22.7% | 3.9% |

*Percent of patients with treatment-related neurotoxicity or cardiotoxicity within 12 months of diagnosis.

### Key Findings

**Neurotoxicity:**
- Highest rates observed in carcinoma lung bronchus NOS (22.7%, n=58) and NSCLC non-squamous cell carcinoma (17.2%, n=59)
- Overall neurotoxicity rate across all lung cancer subtypes: **15.6%** (103/661 patients with chemo)
- Neurotoxicity commonly manifested as fatigue, weakness, and neurological symptoms

**Cardiotoxicity:**
- Cardiotoxicity rates ranged from 0% to 4.5% across subtypes
- Small cell lung cancer showed higher cardiotoxicity (4.5%) compared to NSCLC (~3%)
- Overall cardiotoxicity rate: **3.1%** among all chemo-treated patients

### Clinical Implications

The elevated neurotoxicity rates in non-squamous NSCLC likely reflect the use of more neurotoxic regimens (e.g., platinum-based doublets) in this population. The low but clinically significant cardiotoxicity rates, particularly in small cell lung cancer, warrant cardiac monitoring during and after chemotherapy.
