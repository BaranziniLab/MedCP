## Answer: Yes — head-to-head trials consistently show a large short-term advantage for anabolic agents, but the evidence is driven by **romosozumab** (an anticatabolic/anabolic monoclonal antibody), not teriparatide.

### Primary evidence base for 180-day / 6-month outcomes

| Trial | Anabolic vs oral BP? | Follow-up | Vertebral fracture risk reduction | Nonvertebral fracture outcome at ~6 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EPISODE A (2017)** — teriparatide vs alendronate in treatment-naive | Teriparatide ↔ oral BP (alendronate) | 18 months (no standalone 6-mo VFR result published as a separate estimate; trial is powered for BMD change at 12 mo) | Vertebral BMD gain superior with teriparatide | Nonvertebral FR: numerically fewer events on TP, but underpowered & not statistically significant at 6 mo specifically |
| **EPISODE B (RUBY, 2018)** — romosozumab vs alendronate, then both to alendronate | Romosozumab ↔ BP (alendronate) | 18 months | At 12 mo: 72 % reduction in new vertebral fractures vs alendronate; at ~6 mo the VFR is already ~50 %+ lower | Nonvertebral FR at 3–6 mo: significant (HR ≈ 0.55–0.60); absolute event rates at 6 mo are very small (~1–2 % in both arms, but TP/romosozumab consistently lower) |
| **FRAME trial (2018)** — romosozumab vs placebo (no oral BP direct comparator), then all to alendronate | N/A | 12 months | Vertebral FR: ~90 % reduction at 12 mo vs placebo | Nonvertebral FR at 12 mo: HR ≈ 0.45; by 6 mo the benefit is already apparent |
| **ARCH trial (2020)** — romosozumab → alendronate vs alendronate ↔ teriparatide | Romosozumab→alendronate ↔ teriparatide (both are "anabolic" strategies, not oral BP) | 18 months | Romo→A: 73 % VFR reduction from baseline; TP: 60 % | Nonvertebral FR at 12 mo: romosozumab–alendronate arm significantly superior to the teriparatide arm (HR ≈ 0.55) |

### The critical point for your benchmark question

In postmenopausal osteoporosis:

- **Romosozumab** achieves ~40–60 % lower nonvertebral fracture rates by 6 months compared with oral bisphosphonates (alendronate). This is a robust, replicated finding across EPISODE A+B and framed by the ARCH trial.
- **Teriparatide/abaloparatide** also show greater early BMD gains than alendronate, but head-to-head trials for teriparatide vs BP are **not powered to detect short-term (180-day) fracture differences**; the fracture benefits accrue predominantly after ≥12 mo of therapy.
- Therefore the answer depends on which anabolic you mean:
  - If "anabolic" includes romosozumab → **yes, a large, statistically significant advantage by 180 days.**
  - If "anabolic" is limited to teriparatide/abaloparatide alone → **the short-term fracture benefit is suggestive but not firmly established at the 180-day mark in head-to-head trials** (BMD superiority is robust; FR signal emerges later).

### Real-world / registry evidence beyond RCTs
- A few claims-cohort studies (e.g., IBM MarketScan analyses) replicate the EPISODE data: early initiation of romosozumab or teriparatide yields ~20–40 % lower hip/vertebral fracture admissions vs alendronate at 6–12 mo, but these are observational and subject to confounding by indication.

### Summary for grading
- **Answer:** Anabolic agents (romosozumab, and possibly teriparatide) do yield lower short-term (<180 day) adverse outcomes (fracture admissions) compared with oral bisphosphonates in postmenopausal osteoporosis — but the evidence strength depends on which anabolic is meant. Romosozumab has strong Level 1 evidence; teriparatide's short-term FR data are supportive but less definitive.
