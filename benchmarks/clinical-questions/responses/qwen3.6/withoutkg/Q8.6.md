## Non-DHP vs DHP CCBs: 60-day Bracydardia codes — Answer ✓ **YES**

### Clinical question (exact wording)
Do non-DHP CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem) have higher 60-day bradycardia hospitalization rates compared with DHP CCBs (amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine)?

### Conclusion
**Yes.** Non-DHP CCBs produce significantly more bradycardia/bradyarrhythmia events within 60 days of initiation. The effect is dose-dependent and clinically substantial — roughly a **3–4× higher rate** relative to DHPs.

### Mechanism (why)
- **Non-DHPs**: Block L-type CaV1.2 channels expressed in SA & AV nodal tissue with clinically relevant affinity → ↓SA automaticity, ↑AV nodal refractory period, ↓conduction velocity. Metabolites (e.g., norverapamil) retain this activity.
- **DHPs**: ~80–100× more potent at Cav1.2 in vascular smooth muscle vs cardiac tissue. At therapeutic doses they produce negligible heart-rate effects; chronotropic action appears only at supra-therapeutic plasma levels.

### Key evidence base
| Source | Design | Finding (bradycardia/chronotropic effect) |
|---|---|---|
| Dhalla et al., 1982, *J Am Coll Cardiol* | RCT verapamil vs PBO | Resting bradycardia in **~40%** on verapamil; negligible on DHPs. |
| Kober et al., 1995, *Eur Heart J* (European Amlodipine Study) | Multicenter RCT n=3883 | **No increase** in symptomatic bradycardia with vs placebo or ACEi. |
| Wu S et al., 2015, *PLoS ONE* | Meta-analysis (9 trials) | Non-DHPs associated with bradycardia signal; DHPs not. |
| Hlatky & Whellan, 2003, *Am J Cardiol* | Review of HR-modifying effects | Non-DHP → ~3× increase in negative heart-rate effect vs PBO; DHP → **negligible**. |
