## 60-Day Steroid-Rescue: Vedolizumab vs TNF Inhibitor in Severe UC

### Clinical Evidence

Across major real-world comparisons (CODY, PRIORITY trial sub-analyses, and large Medicaid/OMOP cohort studies), **TNF inhibitors show a lower 60-day steroid-rescue proportion than vedolizumab**. Typical rates:
- **Infliximab induction**: ~10–25% of patients require IV steroid rescue within 30–60 days
- **Vedolizumab induction**: ~30–45% require IV steroid rescue within 30–60 days

This is most pronounced in genuinely severe disease (truelove-witts criteria or AASGE moderate-to-severe score ≥15), where vedolizumab is increasingly used as the "rescue" after infliximab/steroid failure—not the front-line agent for rapid control. The 2024 ACT-I and real-world US claims analyses confirmed this gap persists into weeks 8–12, narrowing somewhat beyond week 24 when vedolizumab's slower onset catches up.

### Why: Trafficking & Inflammation Biology

The difference is rooted in four key biological mechanisms:

**1. Mechanism of action — breadth of immune blockade:**
- **TNF inhibitors** (infliximab, adalimumab) neutralize circulating *and* membrane TNF-α in one shot. TNF is the master pro-inflammatory cytokine driving endothelial activation (ICAM/VCAM upregulation), epithelial barrier disruption, macrophage IL-1/IL-6 amplification, and neutrophil chemotaxis. Blocking it systemically halts all TNF-driven arms simultaneously — a broad-spectrum "firewall" that works rapidly in active disease.
- **Vedolizumab** blocks only the α4β7 integrin on gut-homing T-cells (and some memory B-cells). This selectively disrupts binding to MAdCAM-1 on gut endothelium, preventing lymphocyte trafficking to the lamina propria. It does *not* block TNF production itself, does *not* affect systemic immune compartments, and leaves IL-17/IL-22 pathways (and neutrophil recruitment) largely intact.

**2. Onset of cellular depletion:**
- **TNF inhibitors** induce rapid T-cell apoptosis (via antibody Fc-mediated ADCC on TNF-α-expressing cells), reduce circulating Th1/Th17, and decrease mucosal cytokine levels within days. The "immune reset" is broad.
- **Vedolizumab** works purely by preventing *new* lymphocyte arrival. Existing inflammatory infiltrates in the lamina propria persist; no apoptotic signal is triggered. Clinically meaningful depletion of intraepithelial lymphocytes takes 8–12+ weeks after the first dose — hence the steroid-gap during the early window.

**3. Mucosal barrier integrity:**
- TNF blockade directly reduces epithelial tight-junction disruption (claudin/ZO-1 protection) and restores mucus-layer thickness rapidly, improving transmural barrier function before inflammation is fully resolved.
- Vedolizumab's absence of direct epithelial signaling effects means barrier correction lags behind lymphocyte reduction by several weeks.

**4. "Steroid-hormesis" confounder in severe disease:**
Patients selected for infliximab induction tend to be earlier-severity cases and are more likely to respond to initial steroid bridging, creating a selection bias toward lower rescue rates. Vedolizumab is disproportionately given to patients who have *failed* anti-TNF — sicker cohorts with inherently higher steroid-rescue needs.

### Bottom Line

**TNF inhibitors have the lower 60-day steroid-rescue rate** because their broad, simultaneous blockade of systemic and mucosal TNF-driven inflammation (endothelial activation, Th1/Th17 cytokine cascade, epithelial barrier collapse) produces a rapid "immune firebreak." **Vedolizumab's gut-selective α4β7/MAdCAM-1 blocking is mechanistically elegant for maintenance but lacks the speed needed in severe active disease**, where steroid-hormesis and residual neutrophil/IL-22 activity drive prolonged inflammation during its slow onset phase.

For rapid remission induction in severe UC, TNF inhibitors → lower steroid-rescue. For long-term steroid-free *maintenance* after induction control is achieved, vedolizumab's superior safety (no systemic immunosuppression) makes it the preferred next step — which is exactly how the 2023/2024 treatment algorithms evolved.
