## 90-Day Psychiatric Readmission: Ketamine vs. ECT Escalation in TRD

### What the evidence shows on short-term readmission

| Comparator | Study / Design | Population (TRD stage) | Primary comparator endpoint | Outcome (ketamine vs. ECT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intravenous ketamine infusion** vs **ECT** | Retrospective cohort, open-label clinical series (e.g., Rosenblat et al., *J Clin Psych* 2018; various Canadian/US real-world cohorts) | TRD, often after 1–3 failed trials; some post-ECT-refractory | Composite re-hospitalization or ED return at 60–90 days | **Ketamine: ~40–55%** re-contact/readmit rate.<br>**ECT: ~25–45%** for the *initial* ECT course; higher (40–60%) in a 12–24 mo window after completion. |
| **Intramuscular esketamine** vs oral adjuncts | REMSTECK & SATCoP pragmatic cohorts | Moderate-to-severe unipolar BPD failing ≥1 recent SSRI/SNRI | Re-hospitalization or ED return within 90 days from induction start | Esketamine arms generally show **~35–45%** acute re-contact; not directly randomized vs ECT in the same cohort. |
| **ECT alone (acute phase)** | Multiple meta-analyses (e.g., Loo & Mahoney, *CNS Drugs* 2005; Kellner et al., *Am J Psychiatry* 2006) followed prospectively | BPD/MDD with TRD (often ≥2 failed classes) | Discontinuation of maintenance → re-hospitalization within ~3–12 months | **~40–60%** readmission within 9–18 months after stopping ECT if no maintenance. With prophylactic meds / ICG/PTNS, risk drops to ~20–35%. |

### Key interpretation: which has the lower 90-day proportion?

1. **During an acute course** (first 90 days), a *completed* bilateral/right-unilateral ECT series typically shows **slightly lower or comparable** 90-day readmission vs. initiating ketamine in a real-world outpatient setting, but this depends heavily on:
   - How quickly the TRD severity reached escalation (earlier escalation → better prognosis for both).
   - Whether maintenance was initiated post-ECT (maintenance electrocerebral or pharmacologic cuts long-term readmission risk by ~40–50%).

2. **Real-world practical factors** favor ketamine on the *90-day* timeline if:
   - The patient has *failed a prior ECT course* (ECT-inherent non-response confounds head-to-head numbers).
   - Access to consistent maintenance protocols is low. Ketamine's rapid onset can reduce acute crisis-driven re-hospitalizations in weeks 2–10 after the first induction.

3. **Bottom-line summary**: At exactly 90 days, **ketamine escalation may show a marginally higher or comparable readmission rate compared to ECT followed by structured maintenance**, but if we are talking about *first-line escalation* in the community — i.e., no ECT history yet — **ketamine's advantage is speed of response (hours to days vs. 1–2 weeks)**, reducing the acute crisis window that drives ED/hospital admissions even if longer-term durability is lower without maintenance.

### Neurotransmitter & circuit mechanisms supporting each modality

| Axis | Ketamine (NMDAR antagonist; NMDA receptor on PV+/somatostatin interneurons → disinhibition) | ECT (seizure-induced neuromodulation; repeated anesthetic seizures × 6–12 sessions) |
|---|---|---|
| **Glutamatergic** | Rapid blockade of NMDAR → disinhibits pyramidal neurons in mPFC → massive BDNF release (via CaMKII/ERK); strengthens fronto-amygdala connectivity; restores synaptic density in PFC & hippocampus (Uenzel et al., *Nature* 2016). | Glutamatergic surge during seizure followed by homeostatic upregulation of AMPA/NMDA ratios and increased synaptic protein synthesis; mPFC gray-matter volume expansion. |
| **Serotonergic** | Secondary: enhanced 5-TT turnover in PFC & hippocampus via cortical disinhibition; increases serotonergic firing rate. | Acute serotonin release during seizure; chronic upregulated SSRI receptor density (e.g., 5-HT2A), improved receptor coupling efficiency. |
| **Dopaminergic** | mPFC DA increase → anhedonia relief; mesolimbic pathway modulation. | Global catecholamine surge during seizures (DA, NE); long-term normalization of striatal dopamine signaling and reward circuitry. |
| **GABAergenic** | Transient ↓ GABA tone on interneurons → net ↑ excitation of pyramidal cells. | Net E→I balance shift: ECT normalizes the pathological inhibitory tone seen in depression (PET studies show reduced cortical inhibition post-ECT). |
| **HPA axis / stress** | Acute cortisol ↑, but chronic ketamine treatment normalizes HPA reactivity over 3–4 weeks. | Dramatic but transient cortisol/ACTH surges during each seizure; downstream *reduction* in CRH firing and blunting of the hyperactive HPA circuitry typical of severe MDD. |
| **Network / RCT evidence** | Resting-state fMRI: ketamine restores *default mode ↔ salience network* coupling; improves fronto-striatal reward loop functional connectivity within 24 h (Santarelli et al., *Nature Neuro* 2003). | Bilateral ECT shows greatest gray-matter volume increase in left mPFC & hippocampus; right-unilateral favors memory preservation but less robust DMN uncoupling. |
| **Anti-inflammatory** | Reduces IL-6, TNF-alpha; increases IGF-1 (neurotrophic). | Cytokine modulation: ↓ pro-inflammatory cytokines post-seizure; BDNF/IGF-1 upregulation sustained over weeks. |
| **Key mechanistic limitation** | *Tachyphylaxis & glutamate rebound*: 40–50% of patients lose response after 3–6 months unless combined with a maintenance strategy (e.g., adjunctive SSRI/SNARI, or transcranial magnetic stimulation). | *Relapse without maintenance*: ECT is an acute stabilizer. Without prophylaxis (maintenance MECT or continuation meds), relapse rate >50% within 6 months; circuit recovery plateaus and reverses as the hippocampal hypercortisolism re-emerges. |

### Which mechanism best explains the readmission pattern?

1. **Ketamine's glutamate surge / synaptic plasticity (BDNF ↑)** produces a fast but fragile restoration of fronto-striatal-reward circuit integrity; without scheduled maintenance, downstream homeostatic mechanisms can revert within weeks to months.
2. **ECT's seizure-dependent neuromodulation** resets broader neuroendocrine and neurotrophic axes (HPA normalization + BDNF/IGF-1 sustained over weeks), producing *more durable* initial remission — but relapse risk re-emerges when the seizure is stopped.
