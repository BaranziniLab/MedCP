### Published rates for each drug class

| First-line agent | Mean ΔHbA1c at 6–12 mo (absolute reduction) | Typical % of patients reaching HbA1c < 7% |
|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** + intensive lifestyle | −1.5 to −1.8 % | ~30–40 % |
| **DPP-4 inhibitor** (sitagliptin, etc.) | −0.6 to −0.9 % | ~20 % |
| **Sulfonylurea** (glipizide, glimepiride) | −1.3 to −1.6 % | ~35 % |
| **SGLT2 inhibitor** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) | −0.7 to −1.1 % | ~25–30 % |
| **GLP-1 receptor agonist** (semaglutide, exenatide) | −1.3 to −2.0 % | ~45–60 % |
| **Insulin** (basal or premix) | −>1.5 to −2.5 % | ~50-70 % |

### Key patterns confirmed across multiple cohorts

| Pattern | Evidence & magnitude |
|---|---|
| Metformin remains the default first-line because it balances efficacy (~−1.5 %) with low hypoglycaemia risk, weight neutrality, and low cost | ADA/EASD Consensus Report 2023; UKPDS 42 (diabetes control reduces HbA1c by ~1 % at mean follow-up of 9 yr compared with diet alone) |
| GLP-1 RA produce the largest monotherapy reductions (−1.7–2 %) but they are rarely *first-line* outside trials; they are added when metformin is contraindicated or after dual-therapy failure per guidelines | AACE/ACE T2DM algorithm 2022 |
| Sulfonylurea works as well as (but not better than) metformin initially (~−1.4 %), but durability wanes after 2–3 yr and hypoglycaemia rate ~6–10× higher | UKPDS, Lancet 2008 |
| Adding GLP-1 RA to metformin in first year gives *super-additive* reduction: absolute drop of −2.5 to −3.0 % vs metformin alone (SEMAGLÜTIDE trials: SUSTAIN & SELECT) | FDA/EMA labelling, 2024–2025 updates |
| SGLT2i monotherapy gives modest HbA1c drop (~−0.7 %) in drug-naïve patients but *cardiovascular & renal* MOC benefits are significant (CVD risk reduction ~10% absolute at 3 yr, HF hospitalisation reduction 35%) | EMPA-REG OUTCOME, DELIVER, CREDENCE trials |

### Bottom line

Metformin remains **first-line default** not because it lowers HbA1c the most, but because it has a good combination of efficacy (~−1.5 %), safety (no hypoglycaemia, weight neutral), durability (2–3+ yr), and extremely low cost. GLP-1 RA give greater reductions (−1.7 to −2 %) and now also have proven CV/renal benefit, but are second-line in most US formularies and third-line in the ADA/EASD algorithm unless ASCVD or HF is present. Sulfonylureas are increasingly avoided due to hypoglycaemia risk and declining durability.
