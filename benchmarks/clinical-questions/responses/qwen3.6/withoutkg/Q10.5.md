## GLP-1 RA vs SGLT2i: 6-month weight loss in T2DM with obesity

### Head-to-head evidence
The **SURPASS** programme (semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly) and **DECLARE-TIMI 53 / EMPA-KIDNEY / DELIVER / SELECT** trials collectively show that a GLP-1 RA (especially semaglutide) produces roughly **twice the 6-month weight loss** compared with an SGLT2 inhibitor:

| Drug class | Approximate mean weight loss at month 6 vs baseline (T2DM + overweight/obesity cohort) |
|---|---|
| Semaglutide 1–2.4 mg wks | **−5.5 to −8.5 kg** (SURPASS-1 to SURPASS-4, adjusted for diet/exercise) |
| Dulaglutide 1.45–1.5 mg wks | −3.0 to −4.0 kg (AWARD series) |
| Empagliflozin 10–25 mg daily | **−2.7 to −3.6 kg** (DECLARE-TIMI 53, EMPA-REG OUTCOME sub-analyses) |
| Canagliflozin 100–300 mg daily | −3.1 to −3.8 kg (CANVAS programme) |

**GLP-1 RA wins on weight-loss proportion at 6 months.** In patients with T2DM *and* obesity (as opposed to lean T2DM), semaglutide's effect approaches the magnitude of bariatric surgery (~20–25 % of body weight over ~1 year) whereas SGLT2 inhibitors cap around ~3–5 %.

### Mechanistic/metabolic-signaling explanation
Two distinct, non-overlapping mechanisms explain why GLP-1 RAs outperform SGLT2 inhibitors for fat mass reduction:

| Dimension | GLP-1 RA (semaglutide, liraglutide) | SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary site of action** | Central (area postrema → DMH/ARC/NPVH in hypothalamus) + gastric; peripheral pancreatic β-cells (glucose-dependent insulin secretion) and α-cells (glucagon inhibition) | Proximal renal tubule (SGLT2 = SLC5A2 at the brush border); glycosuria |
| **Energy-balance axis** | Activates pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC)/NPY/AgRP neurons → ↑ α-MSH / MC4R signaling → strong anorexia; delays gastric emptying via vagal afferents (GLP-1R on the nodose ganglion); increases postprandial satiety signals (CCK, PYY, GLP-1 endocrine amplification) | Calorie loss via urinary glucose excretion (~60–80 g/day ≈ 240–320 kcal); mild diuresis + osmotic pressure-mediated natriuresis accounts for the initial kilogram of rapid loss |
| **Fat-mass signaling** | ↑ Central sympathetic outflow to adipose → lipolysis; direct GLP-1R on adipocytes (controversial but supported by mouse knock-in); preserves β-cell mass → lowers fasting insulin (hyperinsulinemia is anti-lipolytic and pro-adipogenic) — lower insulin removes a brake on fat oxidation | SGLT2i does **not** directly reduce central appetite; mild ↑ in ketone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate) may provide some satiety signaling but the effect is small; preserves beta-cell function less robustly than GLP-1RAs, so insulin suppression is modest |
| **Appetite & reward circuitry** | Reduces hedonic/satiety-driven eating by dampening orbitofrontal/amygdala food-cue reactivity (shown in fMRI on liraglutide and semaglutide). This appetite-suppression effect is absent with SGLT2i. | Not observed. Appetitive drive is essentially unchanged; weight loss is entirely passive caloric deficit (you do not "feel" full, you just lose glucose-calories). |
| **Lean-body-mass preservation** | Greater lean-fat mass ratio of weight lost (~70–75 % fat vs lean in SURPASS) | Slightly better lean-mass preservation but total loss is smaller; the absolute fat-free-mass gain is negligible. |

### Bottom line
In T2DM with obesity the **GLP-1 RA pathway wins** on 6-month weight-loss proportion because it:

1. **Actively suppresses appetite** through central GLP-1R → MC4R signaling (the dominant driver of total energy deficit).
2. **Lowers fasting insulin**, releasing lipolysis and fat oxidation.
3. **Delays gastric emptying**, amplifying postprandial fullness signals.

SGLT2 inhibitors produce weight loss only through a passive osmotic mechanism (~250 kcal/day from glycosuria) without appetite suppression, so their 6-month effect plateaus at ~3–4 kg regardless of obesity status.
