## Pediatric Obesity-Mental Health Comorbidity

### Most common co-occurring diagnoses
1. **Anxiety disorders** — particularly generalized anxiety, social anxiety; prevalence 28-40%
2. **Major depressive disorder** — second most common; prevalence 20-30%; girls 3x risk vs boys
3. **ADHD** — strongest behavioral association; 61% of obese children compared to 57% normal-weight (Klein et al., 2018)
4. **Eating disorders** — binge eating disorder, night eating syndrome; particularly in adolescents
5. **Suicidal ideation/attempts** — dose-response with BMI percentile [NHS data]

This comorbidity pattern is bidirectional: mental illness can drive hyperphagia (SSRI/gain-promoting meds, emotion-driven eating) while obesity drives internalizing symptoms via stigma and HPA-axis dysregulation.

### Shared appetite/stress pathways
| Pathway | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **HPA axis** | Chronic stress → cortisol ↑ in hypothalamus (NPY/AgRP neurons) → orexigenic drive + adiposity [Kahn et al., 2015] | Obese children show flatter diurnal cortisol slopes; stress-reactivity predicts weight gain |
| **CRH** | Hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone blunts satiety, promotes emotional eating [Inagaki et al., 2012] | CRH₁R antagonist reduced bingeing in murine models |
| **Ghrelin/Leptin resistance** | Hyperinsulinemia impairs leptin transport across BBB; ghrelin becomes uncoupled from satiety [Coi et al., 2005] | Obese youth have elevated fasting ghrelin but blunted post-prandial suppression |
| **Dopamine reward** | VTA-NAc sensitization → preference for energy-dense foods; comorbidity with ADHD and anhedonia [Stice, 2001] | fMRI shows reduced striatal activation to food cues in obesity + depression |
| **Inflammatory cytokines** | Adipokine (IL-6, TNF-α) cross BBB → microglial activation → serotonin dysregulation → depression | Correlation r=0.34 between hs-CRP and depressive symptoms [Janssens et al., 2015] |

### Bidirectional risk structure
Normal-weight children with anxiety/depression have ~1.3x relative risk of obesity by young adulthood; obese youth have ~2-3x odds of subsequent internalizing disorders. The strongest single confounder is **adolescent female sex**.
