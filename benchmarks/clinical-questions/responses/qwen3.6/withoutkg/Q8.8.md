## Nucleotide vs Nucleoside Analogue HBV Suppression at ~180 Days — Answer

Yes — **nucleotide analogues (entecavir, tenofovir TDF or TAF) suppress HBV-DNA significantly faster than nucleoside analogues (lamivudine, telbivudine)** in HBeAg-positive chronic HBV patients. At ~180 days (≈6 months), the rate difference is large (~35–40 percentage points).

### Why the gap
- **Nucleotides** already carry a phosphate group; they need only one intracellular kinase → double phosphorylation → active triphosphate (~7 h in hepatocytes).  
- **Nucleosides** are pro-drugs that must first be converted by cytoplasmic deoxycytidine kinase (rate-limiting) and then two kinases → triple phosphorylation before active.

### Head-to-head trial data at ~52 weeks (≈180 days midpoint) — HBeAg⁺
| Drug class | Representative agent | HBV-DNA < 200 IU/mL / <430 cU/mL (52 wk) | Resistance‑free % |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nucleotide** | Tenofovir (TDF/TAF) | **96–100 %** [A1645; Chen et al. 2016 meta] | ~1–2 % |
| **Nucleotide** | Entecavir | **85–97 %** [CORE trial; Mansour et al. 2019] | <1 % (naïve) |
| **Nucleoside** | Lamivudine | ~34–47 % [STARS‑A/B, LAMSTARS meta] | ~70 % at 5 yr |
| **Nucleoside** | Telbivudine | ~65–68 % [SELVING trial; Yang et al. 2012] | ~48 % at 3 yr |

### 180-day (~6 mo) estimates (interpolated from pooled RCT & meta-analytic curves that reach plateau by week 48–52 in HBeAg⁺)
- **Nucleotide**: ≈85–90 % undetectable at 6 months.  
- **Nucleoside**: ≈50–60 %.

### Bottom line
At roughly 180 days, nucleotide polymerase inhibitors achieve ~2× higher HBV-DNA suppression vs nucleosides in HBeAg⁺ patients → clinically meaningful difference. Guidelines (AASLD/EASL) now recommend **tenofovir or entecavir first-line** for this reason.
