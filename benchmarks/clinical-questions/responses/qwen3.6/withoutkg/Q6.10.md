## Sickle Cell Disease — Most Frequent Infections

From clinical cohorts (e.g., SS disease registries, Cooperative Study of SCA):

| Infection type | Clinical significance |
|---|---|
| **Pneumococcal sepsis / pneumonia** (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*) | Leading infectious cause of mortality in children < 5 yrs with HbSS; >80% of fatal pneumococcal cases are serotypes 1, 5, or 3 |
| **Salmonella osteomyelitis** | *S. enterica* (especially non-typhoidal *Salmonella*) is the most common cause of focal bone infection in SCD (≈30–75% of osteomyelitis cases); *S. aureus* and *Kingella kingae* are also frequent |
| **Hib (Haemophilus influenzae type b)** | Historically important; now less common due to vaccination but still seen in under-immunized populations |
| **Varicella/zoster** | Severe primary varicella pneumonia risk is markedly elevated, particularly in adults with active disease |
| **Community-acquired respiratory pathogens** (influenza A/B, RSV, *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*) | Major triggers of acute chest syndrome (ACS), the leading cause of SCA-related hospitalization and second-leading cause of mortality |

## Immune/Hemolysis Mechanisms Linking to Susceptibility

These aren't just "immunocompromised" — SCD impairs multiple coordinated defense layers:

1. **Functional asplenia** (by age 2–3 in most HbSS patients)
   - Repeated splenic infarction → autosplenectomy → loss of marginal zone B cells
   - Eliminates IgM memory B cells required for T-independent polysaccharide responses (pneumococcus, Hib have荚膜多糖 that depend on this pathway)
   - Loss of red pulp macrophage clearance of opsonized particles

2. **Complement depletion / consumptive process** (>70% of HbSS vs ≈30% HS show low factor B and CH50)
   - Chronic hemolysis consumes C3b, degrading the classical pathway's initial step for opsonization
   - Yet **C5a remains elevated** (not depleted), so terminal complement-driven inflammation is paradoxically increased rather than decreased

3. **Efferocytosis defect** — dying/sickling RBC clearance requires splenic macrophage recognition of **annexin A1** on the erythrocyte surface. In SCD, the damaged membrane cannot present this signal properly → impaired efferocytosis → secondary necrosis with DAMP release → neutrophil activation

4. **NO depletion pathway**
   - Free hemoglobin released from lysed RBCs scavenges nitric oxide (NO) at high affinity
   - NO is a microbiostatic agent for many intracellular and gram-negative organisms; its loss removes this first-line antimicrobial defense

5. **NETosis-driven pathology** — sickled erythrocytes directly induce neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation via TLR4/9 and caspase-11, which:
   - Provides iron for bacterial growth when NET-bound ferritin is degraded by bacterial proteases
   - Drives lung endothelial damage, fueling ACS episodes

6. **Iron dysregulation** — chronic hemolysis causes elevated serum iron despite anemia, promoting bacterial siderophore-mediated growth (iron acquisition is a critical virulence factor for the pathogens above)

In summary: SCD patients aren't broadly immunocompromised; their susceptibility is highly specific to encapsulated bacteria and organisms handled by asplenic/complement-dependent defenses, amplified by hemolysis-driven complement consumption and NO loss rather than complement deficiency alone in most patients.
