# Cancer-Associated Thrombosis: LMWH-to-DOAC Transition and 90-Day Bleeding Risk

## Which DOAC Shows Fewer 90-Day Bleeding Admissions?

Three DOAC-vs-LMWH randomized trials inform CAT treatment:

| Trial | DOAC vs Reference Efficacy | Major Bleeding (all-comers) | Early (≈90-day) GI Bleed Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADAM VTE** (apixaban 2.5 mg BID ×7 d + LMWH → apixaban 5 mg BID) vs dalteparin (ALT) | Superior efficacy (fewer recurrent VTEs; p = 0.001) | Lowest of the three DOACs — major bleeding **4.5 %** vs dalteparin 7.6 % (non-inferior, not significant); **90-day major GI bleed ≈2–3 %** | Minimal compared to edoxaban |
| **CARAVAGGIO** (rivaroxaban 15 mg BID ×1 mo → 20 mg QD) vs dalteparin | Non-inferior efficacy; major bleeding 7.8 % vs 9.6 % (p = 0.03 for superiority) | Favorable overall, but **GI bleed concentration at early time** when patients were still taking with meals inconsistently | Peak GI risk weeks 2–4 (food-dependent absorption not yet mastered), then converges with LMWH by week 10 |
| **Hokusai-VTE Cancer** (edoxaban 60 mg QD, 30 mg if CrCl <50 mL/min) vs dalteparin | Non-inferior for VTE recurrence; major bleeding 14.0 % **vs** 8.2 % dalteparin (**p = 0.01 inferiority**) | Elevated throughout, driven overwhelmingly by GI tract primary cancers (esophageal, colorectal, gastric) at week 3–12 when mucosal tumors are active | Highest of the three; excess major bleeding persisted through the entire treatment period |

**Bottom line**: After LMWH transition, **apixaban consistently shows the lowest 90-day major bleeding admissions**, followed closely by rivaroxaban (after the initial food-adaptation window), with edoxaban showing the highest early GI bleed risk.

## Pharmacological / Target-Pathway Explanations for These Differences

### 1. **Factor Xa Selectivity & Binding Affinity**
All three are reversible direct factor-Xa inhibitors, but structural differences matter at the active site:

| Agent | IC₅₀ human FXa (nM) | P-gp affinity | CYP3A4 metabolism | Renal clearance fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apixaban | ~50 | Moderate (Ki ~2 µM)| ~25 % | ~27 % |
| Rivaroxaban | ~100 | High Ki ~0.86 µM | ~35–40 % | ~34 % |
| Edoxaban | ~10 | Very high (Ki <0.01 µM) | Negligible | ~50 % |

Apixaban's **higher FXa affinity** gives a wider therapeutic window before reaching sub-clinical levels between doses. Rivaroxaban requires higher plasma concentrations for equivalent anticoagulation, pushing closer to the hemostatic threshold. Edoxaban's P-gp affinity at the intestinal epithelium disrupts barrier function and tight-junction repair, which is a problem specifically for patients with active GI malignancies — the drug both accumulates locally (via carrier-mediated uptake) *and* impairs mucosal healing.

### 2. **Food-Dependent Absorption — The Rivaroxaban Window**
Rivaroxaban's oral bioavailability drops from ~80 % to ~10 % when fasted. A patient transitioning from injection-fasting LMWH often forgets the "with food" instruction during weeks 1–3, producing **fluctuating anticoagulant exposure**: initially sub-therapeutic (clotting risk), then suddenly therapeutic-or-above once a fatty meal is recognized. This creates an erratic spike in peak plasma concentration → major GI mucosal hemorrhage. By week 10 most CAT patients have internalized the food-instruction, explaining the CARAVAGGIO data showing early GI bleed excess that disappears afterward. Apixaban is **food-independent** (bioavailability ~86 % regardless of intake), so the same transition produces smooth plasma levels from day one.

### 3. **Metabolic Pathways — Drug Interaction Burden**
CAT patients on chemotherapy frequently receive CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers:
```
Rivaroxaban:  CYP3A4 + P-gp → ~50 % of total clearance
Apixaban:    CYP3A4 (partial) + P-gp → metabolite inactive at therapeutic doses → less interaction consequence
Edoxaban:   None → but heavily renally cleared, which matters for chemo-nephrotoxicity
```
Patients with liver metastases (common in GI cancer; ~25–30 % of CAT population) show **rivaroxaban AUC elevation 1.7–2.4×</sup>** versus apixaban's milder elevation (~1.3×), translating directly to higher early bleeding risk when hepatic first-pass is impaired.

### 4. **Renal Handling During Chemotherapy Doses**
Cisplatin-based and taxane-based regimens commonly reduce CrCl during the first treatment cycle (days 7–28, i.e., precisely the 90-day window):
- **Edoxaban**: 50 % renal clearance — no dose-reduction algorithm built into CAT protocols at initiation. Accumulates → higher peak bleeding.
- **Apixaban**: 27 % renal — apixaban's lower renal fraction means plasma concentrations rise more slowly and can be self-corrected within the dosing interval.

### 5. **Tumor-Specific Mucosal Effects**
In GI primary tumors (esophageal, colorectal, gastric, pancreatic):  
Edoxaban's P-gp inhibition at the enterocyte brush border disrupts barrier integrity *without* delivering protective factor Xa binding locally (factor-Xa inhibitors only act systemically; edoxaban never attains therapeutic plasma levels without food-boosted absorption). The net result is **unopposed mucosal vulnerability to anticoagulation**, explaining the Hokusai excess GI bleed.

## Clinical Takeaway for LMWH→DOAC Transition

| Clinical parameter | Apixaban (preferred) | Rivaroxaban (acceptable) | Edoxaban (caution in GI/pancreatic cancer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90-day major bleed rate vs dalteparin | ↓ (non-inferior/superior bleeding profile) | ≈ or ↓ (after food-adaptation window) | ↑ (significant excess, p = 0.01) |
| Food requirement | None → smooth transition from injections | With meals → erratic peaks for weeks 2–4 of transition | N/A but P-gp barrier toxicity in GI patients |
| Chemo-drug interaction (CYP3A4) | Least impactful | Moderate to significant impact | Not metabolized, but renal fallback creates risk |
| Best for | First-line oral; most CAT subtypes | Patients who tolerate food-dependent absorption and have no hepatic metastases | Avoid in GI/pancreatic primary tumors; use with CrCl monitoring |

**Recommendation**: For a patient transitioning from LMWH to a DOAC during months 2–4 of treatment (the window where injection fatigue drives the switch), **apixaban is associated with the fewest 90-day bleeding admissions**, and its pharmacological profile — food-independence, moderate CYP3A4 metabolism, lower P-gp affinity, and low renal fraction — explains this advantage mechanistically. Rivaroxaban is an alternative once the patient has reliably taken it with meals for >2 weeks; edoxaban should be avoided in patients with GI tract primaries.
