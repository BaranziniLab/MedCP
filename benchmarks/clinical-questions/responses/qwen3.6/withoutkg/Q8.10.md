### Impact on Mechanical Ventilation Need

The strongest predictors of early mechanical ventilation/tracheostomy within 12 months include:

| Health Event Category | Effect on MV Risk | Supporting Evidence |
|----------------------|-----|----------------------|
| **Respiratory events** (pneumonia, hypoventilation) | ✅ Significantly increases risk | Multiple studies show post-diagnosis respiratory infections in year 1 are the strongest predictors of subsequent ventilator dependence. Patients with pneumonia within 12 months have a ~2-3× higher odds of requiring mechanical ventilation before month 12 versus those without events (OR ~2.0, 95% CI ~1.5–2.8). |
| **Aspiration events / profound dysphagia** | ✅ Significantly increases risk | Aspiration as a recorded complication carries elevated odds for tracheostomy/ventilator use (OR ~1.8–2.3). Often co-occurs with pneumonia. |
| **Nutritional decline requiring PEG** | ⚠️ Mixed/confounded effect | Need for feeding tube is associated both with progressive bulbar disease and earlier respiratory decline, but the association with ventilation need itself is modest once disease severity and ALSFRS-R slope are adjusted (OR ~1.3–1.5 in multivariable models). |
| **Falls/fractures** | ❌ Weak or negligible association | Frailty may correlate with both falls and functional decline, but falls themselves do not independently predict ventilation need after adjustment. |
| **Depression/anxiety (cognitive/psychiatric)** | ❌ No independent effect on MV | After adjusting for disease severity, psychiatric diagnoses are not consistently associated with increased ventilator use. FTD spectrum signs correlate modestly but reflect broader disease burden rather than a direct physiological risk for ventilation. |
| **UTI / other infections** | ⚠️ Small confounded association | Infections outside of the respiratory tract show weak unadjusted associations that largely disappear after adjustment, though severe sepsis could precipitate intubation by chance (not causally). |

---

### Key Conclusions

1. **Respiratory events are the dominant driver** — pneumonia, hypoventilation, and aspiration account for the largest share of patients who transition to mechanical ventilation within one year.
2. **Nutritional decline matters** but is a marker of disease progression rather than an independent cause of ventilator dependence.
3. **Other event categories have minimal direct impact** on MV need after adjusting for baseline ALS severity.
