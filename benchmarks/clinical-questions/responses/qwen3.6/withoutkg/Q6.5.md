Cocaine and amphetamine/stimulant-use disorders are the next most common drug SUDs after alcohol. Tobacco/nicotine dependence has the highest prevalence but is sometimes reported separately due to social normalisation.

### Subgroup patterns
- **Rapid-cycling and mixed features** → higher stimulants & nicotine co-occurrence.
- **BD-I vs BD-II** → BD-I shows markedly higher cocaine/amphetamine use; BD-II shows more cannabis/alcohol predominance.
- **Age of onset** — early-onset bipolar is strongly associated with adolescent alcohol/cannabis initiation, which worsens course and suicide risk.

## Neurotransmitter Systems Linking Bipolar Disorder and SUD

The overlap is not coincidental — multiple neurotransmitter systems are disrupted in both conditions:

### 1. Dopaminergic system (ventral striatum / reward circuitry)
- **Core link**: Both bipolar mania/hypomania and substance intoxication involve heightened dopamine transmission, particularly mesolimbic DA release from VTA → nucleus accumbens.
- Chronic stimulants (cocaine, amphetamine) directly increase synaptic DA via DAT blockade or VMAT2 reversal — mirroring the manic state's hyperdopaminergic profile.
- Antiretroviral and mood-stabilizer responses often converge on D2/D3 receptor down-regulation as a shared treatment target.

### 2. Glutamatergic system (cortico-limbic circuits)
- Post-mortem and neuroimaging studies confirm **reduced cortical glutamate transporters (GLT-1, GLAST)** in both bipolar disorder and addiction models.
- Chronic substance exposure sensitizes AMPA/NMDA receptors in the nucleus accumbens → long-term potentiation of drug-associated memories (the neural basis of craving). Bipolar patients have reduced prefrontal cortical glutamate modulation, making this loop harder to disrupt.

### 3. GABAergic system
- Both conditions show **reduced GABA synthesis** and expression of GAD67/GAT-1 in cortex, hippocampus, and anterior cingulate.
- This creates a shared vulnerability: impaired inhibitory tone → disinhibited reward seeking + emotional lability. Valproate (which increases brain GABA) has some cross-prophylactic efficacy against both manic symptoms and alcohol craving.

### 4. Serotonergic system
- **5-HT dysregulation** is present in depression, impulsivity, and suicide risk across bipolar disorder and SUD populations; SSRIs have limited monotherapy efficacy (risk of mania induction).
- The CRF/hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis is closely tied to 5-HT signaling — chronic stress/CRF up-regulation drives both manic relapse and drug-seeking behavior.
- **Vasopressin** and **oxytocin** pathways are downstream from this neuroendocrine axis and show shared impairment in emotional regulation deficits.

### 5. Endocannabinoid system (often overlooked)
- **CB1 receptor down-regulation** is documented after chronic cannabis exposure, and parallels the altered endocannabinoid tone seen in bipolar depression/mania phases.
- This creates a biological explanation for why some patients use cannabis to "self-medicate" anxiety or sleep — it masks symptoms but worsens long-term course and cognitive outcomes.

---

### Cross-Summary

The convergence is structural: **dopamine (reward/mania), glutamate (excitation/sensitization), GABA (inhibition/restraint), serotonin (impulse control/affect regulation), and the endocannabinoid system** are all perturbed in both bipolar disorder and SUD. This is why co-occurrence drives worse treatment response, higher relapse rates, and greater suicide risk — they share pathophysiology, not just comorbidity.
