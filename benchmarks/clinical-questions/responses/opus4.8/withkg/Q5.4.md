## Answer

Among adults (≥18 at first diagnosis) with newly diagnosed T2DM in this OMOP database, the three most commonly started **first non-insulin (outpatient oral/injectable) medications** are:

1. **Metformin** — 18,963 patients
2. **Glipizide** (sulfonylurea) — 3,365 patients
3. **Empagliflozin** (SGLT2 inhibitor) — 2,524 patients

### Important caveat on "first medication"
The answer depends on how you define the first drug:

- **If you count *any* antidiabetic exposure at/after diagnosis** (my first query), the top three are **insulin aspart (19,957), metformin (12,421), and insulin glargine (8,366)**. These insulins overwhelmingly reflect **inpatient sliding-scale/basal insulin administered around the diagnosis encounter**, not a chronic outpatient regimen — a well-known artifact of EHR data.
- **Excluding insulin** (the query above), the picture matches real-world initiation guidelines: **metformin dominates first-line**, followed distantly by a sulfonylurea (glipizide) and an SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin).

### Method
- T2DM cohort: SNOMED "Type 2 diabetes mellitus" (concept 201826) + all descendants via `concept_ancestor`; earliest `condition_start_date` per person taken as diagnosis; restricted to age ≥18 at that date.
- First drug: earliest `drug_exposure` mapping (via `concept_ancestor`) to an ATC A10 "Drugs used in diabetes" ingredient (concept 21600712) on/after the diagnosis date, ranked per patient.
