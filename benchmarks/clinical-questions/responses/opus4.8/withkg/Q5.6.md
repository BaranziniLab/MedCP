## Bottom line

By **individual class**, the most commonly used antidepressants in older adults are:

1. **SSRIs** — the single largest true class (~26,800 patients). SSRIs (e.g., sertraline, escitalopram, citalopram) are the guideline-recommended first-line choice in geriatric depression.
2. **Trazodone** — ~18,400 patients, though much of this reflects low-dose use as a sleep aid rather than for depression.
3. **Mirtazapine** — ~11,800 patients (also frequently used for sleep/appetite in frail elderly).
4. **SNRIs** (duloxetine + venlafaxine + desvenlafaxine ≈ 14,100 patients combined) — duloxetine especially, often for comorbid neuropathic/musculoskeletal pain.
5. **TCAs** (~8,200) — declining use, relevant because they are on the **Beers Criteria** as potentially inappropriate in older adults due to anticholinergic burden.

**Caveat:** counts reflect prescribing/exposure records and include off-label indications (trazodone/mirtazapine for insomnia). If you want this filtered to a depression diagnosis, or expressed as prevalence per person-year, I can refine the query.
