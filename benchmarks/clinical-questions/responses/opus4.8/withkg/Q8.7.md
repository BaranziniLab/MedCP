## Answer: No — not in this database.

In this OMOP EHR cohort of chronic-schizophrenia patients starting an antipsychotic, typical antipsychotics did **not** show a higher 12-month parkinsonism rate than atypicals. The observed rate was numerically *lower* for typicals (1.3% vs 2.2%), the opposite of the textbook expectation that first-generation agents cause more drug-induced parkinsonism/EPS.

**This result is not reliable evidence, and should not be read as contradicting the known pharmacology.** The reasons are all about the data, not the biology:

- **Tiny numerators.** 1 event vs 6 events. A single case shifts the typical-group rate by ~1.3 points. There is no statistical power here (Fisher's exact ≈ n.s.); the confidence intervals overlap heavily.
- **Severe confounding by indication.** Atypicals are the modern first-line choice, so the 3.5:1 atypical:typical split means the two groups are not comparable. Clinicians often reserve typicals (e.g. haloperidol) or switch *to* atypicals precisely because of EPS risk — channeling that biases against the expected direction.
- **EHR under-capture of EPS.** Mild drug-induced parkinsonism is frequently not coded as a discrete condition; it may live in notes or be managed by dose change/anticholinergic without a diagnosis code. Coded-condition ascertainment undercounts the true rate in both arms.
- **No adjustment.** These are crude incidences with no control for age, dose, exposure duration, or concomitant medication.

### Cohort definition used
- **Chronic schizophrenia**: SNOMED "Chronic schizophrenia" and chronic subtypes (paranoid, catatonic, residual, etc.) + descendants.
- **Index**: first antipsychotic exposure on/after the schizophrenia diagnosis; classified typical vs atypical by ingredient (35 same-day mixed starts excluded).
- **Outcome**: incident neuroleptic-induced parkinsonism, extrapyramidal disease/signs/movements, acute akathisia, neuroleptic-induced dystonia, neuroleptic adverse reaction (+ descendants) within 365 days, excluding anyone with such a code at/before index.
- **Follow-up**: ≥365 days of observation after index required.

Bottom line: the literature-supported answer is that typicals *do* carry higher parkinsonism/EPS risk, but **this dataset is too small and too confounded to demonstrate it** — and here it doesn't.
