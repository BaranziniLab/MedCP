**Caveats:** small, non‑randomized cohorts; different baseline eosinophil distributions reflect channeling (IL‑5 agents are prescribed for the eosinophilic phenotype, omalizumab for the allergic/IgE phenotype), so this is not a head‑to‑head efficacy comparison. Reslizumab is essentially anecdotal here (n=1).

### How the mechanisms differ

- **Mepolizumab, reslizumab — anti‑IL‑5 (ligand neutralization):** bind circulating IL‑5, the key cytokine for eosinophil maturation, recruitment, and survival. Removing the survival signal starves eosinophils → counts fall ~50–80% over weeks, but a residual population persists.
- **Benralizumab — anti‑IL‑5Rα (receptor + ADCC):** binds the α‑subunit of the IL‑5 receptor on eosinophils/basophils; its afucosylated Fc recruits NK cells to trigger **antibody‑dependent cell‑mediated cytotoxicity**, actively killing the cells rather than just withdrawing their growth factor. This produces the most **rapid and near‑complete depletion**.
- **Omalizumab — anti‑IgE (upstream, allergic axis):** binds free IgE, blocking IgE–FcεRI engagement on mast cells and basophils and downregulating FcεRI. It interrupts the allergic cascade **upstream** of type‑2 inflammation; any eosinophil reduction is indirect and modest — hence the flat 90‑day eosinophil trajectory above.

**Bottom line:** IL‑5/IL‑5Rα biologics are eosinophil‑directed and achieve markedly greater 90‑day eosinophil suppression (benralizumab deepest via ADCC), whereas anti‑IgE acts on the upstream allergic pathway and leaves eosinophil counts largely unchanged. Choice between them is driven by phenotype (eosinophilic vs. allergic/IgE‑high), not by eosinophil‑suppression depth alone.
