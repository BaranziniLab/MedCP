**Bottom line:** On a like-for-like (hospitalized-index) basis, **ketamine/esketamine has the lower 90-day psychiatric readmission proportion — 24.3% vs ECT's 30.0%.** The eye-catching naïve gap (61% vs 20%) is a care-setting artifact and should not be reported as a treatment effect.

**Clinical result (OMOP EHR)**
- Cohort: MDD diagnosis + escalation therapy. Ketamine/esketamine index n=6709; ECT n=622.
- Naïve (any-setting index): ketamine 61.0% vs ECT 19.8% — misleading, because most ketamine/esketamine is outpatient and never has an index discharge.
- Matched (escalation during inpatient stay, readmission from discharge): **ketamine 24.3% (1235/5076) vs ECT 30.0% (45/150)** → ketamine favored.

**Mechanism (knowledge graph + pharmacology)**
- KG confirms ketamine/esketamine = **NMDA-receptor antagonist** that **treats MDD**.
- Circuit cascade: NMDA blockade on GABAergic interneurons → disinhibition of PFC pyramidal neurons → glutamate surge → AMPA activation → BDNF → mTORC1 → rapid synaptogenesis/spine restoration → restored PFC–limbic connectivity. This fast, circuit-specific plasticity plausibly stabilizes patients sooner than ECT's seizure-mediated, less targeted neuroplasticity.

**Caveats:** retrospective and unadjusted; ECT hospitalized subgroup is small (n=150) and typically sicker. A defensible estimate needs propensity matching (age, severity, comorbidity, prior admissions, LOS) and a competing-risks time-to-readmission model rather than a fixed 90-day proportion.
