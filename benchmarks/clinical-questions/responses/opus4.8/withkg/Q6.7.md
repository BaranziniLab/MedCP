## Mineral-metabolism (CKD-MBD) mechanisms linking the two
The disordered calcium–phosphate–PTH–vitamin D–FGF23 axis ("CKD-MBD") is a central driver:

1. **Phosphate retention.** Falling GFR reduces phosphate excretion → hyperphosphatemia. Phosphate directly induces **vascular smooth-muscle cells to transdifferentiate into osteoblast-like cells** (Runx2/Msx2 upregulation), driving medial arterial calcification → arterial stiffness, elevated pulse pressure, LVH.

2. **FGF23 elevation.** Rises early to defend phosphate excretion. High FGF23 is independently associated with **LVH and heart failure** — proposed off-target, klotho-independent effect on cardiomyocytes (FGFR4 → calcineurin/NFAT hypertrophic signaling) — and with mortality.

3. **Klotho deficiency.** The failing kidney loses α-klotho, removing a vasculoprotective factor and worsening phosphate handling and calcification.

4. **Vitamin D deficiency (low calcitriol).** Reduced 1α-hydroxylation → low active vitamin D, contributing to secondary hyperparathyroidism and possibly to myocardial and vascular dysfunction.

5. **Secondary hyperparathyroidism.** High PTH promotes myocardial fibrosis, LVH, and vascular remodeling; also mobilizes calcium × phosphate product favoring calcification.

6. **Calcium–phosphate product and calcification balance.** Elevated Ca×P plus loss of inhibitors (fetuin-A, matrix Gla protein) tips the balance toward ectopic calcification of vessels and heart valves.

Net effect: the mineral axis converts CKD into an accelerated **vascular calcification + LV hypertrophy/fibrosis** phenotype, explaining the excess coronary disease, heart failure, valvular disease, and arrhythmic/sudden death seen in these patients.
