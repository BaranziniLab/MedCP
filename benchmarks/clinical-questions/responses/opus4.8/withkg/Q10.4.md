## Receptor signaling that could support the difference

The knowledge graph shows the two classes differ mainly in **what they hit *beyond* β1**:

- **Highly β1-selective agents** (bisoprolol, metoprolol) bind essentially only **ADRB1** with meaningful affinity — pure blockade of β1→Gs→adenylyl cyclase→cAMP/PKA. They spare α1 and β2.
- **Less/non-selective agents** engage a broader adrenergic panel:
  - **Carvedilol** binds **ADRB1, ADRB2, ADRB3 and ADRA1A/1D, ADRA2A/2B/2C** — i.e. it adds **α1-adrenergic blockade** (afterload reduction / vasodilation) on top of β-blockade.
  - **Nebivolol** (β1-selective but vasodilatory) and carvedilol are associated with additional vasodilatory signaling.

Plausible mechanistic link to fewer readmissions: the α1-blockade / vasodilator activity of carvedilol-type agents lowers afterload and may blunt maladaptive α1- and β2-mediated remodeling signaling in the failing, post-infarct ventricle — the pharmacologic rationale behind carvedilol's mortality/hospitalization benefit in HFrEF trials (e.g., COPERNICUS, CIBIS-II for bisoprolol as the selective comparator).

**Important caveats.** This is an unadjusted, observational proportion from one OMOP database — no adjustment for age, baseline severity, EF, dosing, or confounding by indication (sicker patients are often channeled to specific agents), and the β1-selective group is dominated by metoprolol whose *tartrate* formulation lacks HFrEF benefit. The receptor-target data explain a *biologically plausible* mechanism but do not establish that the observed readmission gap is causal. Treat this as hypothesis-generating.
