## Short answer
Mechanistically, the **gut-selective integrin blocker (vedolizumab)** is the class expected to spare steroids in maintenance UC. But in this EHR cohort the **naive 60-day steroid-rescue proportion is actually *lower* for TNF inhibitors** — a result that is confounded and should not be read as vedolizumab being inferior in severe/acute disease.

## Clinical data (this database)
Severe/active UC patients, first-ever biologic, systemic steroid started within 60 days after initiation:

| Class | Patients | Rescued ≤60d | Proportion |
|---|---|---|---|
| TNF inhibitor (infliximab/adalimumab/golimumab) | 1,762 | 1,058 | **60.0%** |
| Vedolizumab (α4β7) | 478 | 395 | **82.6%** |

So TNFi shows the lower crude rescue proportion here. Read this cautiously:
- **Channeling/confounding by indication:** vedolizumab is frequently used later-line and in patients who already failed anti-TNF, and its onset of action is slower — both inflate early steroid overlap.
- **Acuity mismatch:** for *severe/hospitalized acute* UC, infliximab (with its rapid, systemic anti-inflammatory effect) is the established rescue agent, whereas gut-selective vedolizumab is slower and less suited to the acute steroid-refractory setting. The 60-day window favors the faster-acting drug.
- This is an association from observational data, not a randomized head-to-head; no adjustment for line-of-therapy, disease severity, or baseline steroid dependence was applied.

## Trafficking / inflammation biology (knowledge graph)
The two classes act on different points of the inflammatory cascade, which explains both the mechanistic rationale and the acuity caveat:

- **Gut-selective integrin (vedolizumab → α4β7 integrin, `ITGA4`/`ITGB7`):** blocks the α4β7–MAdCAM-1 (`MADCAM1`) adhesion axis. KG confirms these genes drive *leukocyte tethering or rolling*, *leukocyte adhesion to vascular endothelial cell*, *lymphocyte migration*, and *immune response in gut-associated lymphoid tissue*. Vedolizumab prevents gut-homing lymphocyte extravasation specifically at intestinal endothelium — highly gut-selective, but it acts **upstream** on recruitment, so it takes weeks to deplete the mucosal infiltrate and does little for cells already in the tissue. That slow kinetics is exactly why it underperforms in the acute 60-day rescue window.
- **TNF inhibitor (→ `TNF`):** neutralizes a downstream effector cytokine, driving rapid, systemic suppression of mucosal inflammation, apoptosis of lamina propria T cells, and fast symptomatic control — better matched to severe/acute steroid-sparing over a short horizon, at the cost of systemic immunosuppression.

**Bottom line:** biology predicts gut-selective integrin blockade is the more *targeted* (and long-term steroid-sparing) mechanism, but its upstream, slow anti-trafficking action means TNF inhibition wins on *early* (60-day) steroid rescue in severe UC — consistent with the crude EHR numbers, though those numbers are confounded by line-of-therapy and severity.
