## Why SCD patients are susceptible

**1. Functional asplenia (the dominant mechanism).** Repeated sickling and microvascular occlusion in the splenic sinusoids cause infarction and progressive autosplenectomy, often by early childhood in HbSS. The spleen is the main site for clearing **encapsulated bacteria** (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *N. meningitidis*, *Salmonella*) and for producing opsonizing IgM and marginal-zone B-cell responses. Its loss is why pneumonia/pneumococcal sepsis and *Salmonella* infections are classic — and why penicillin prophylaxis and pneumococcal vaccination are standard of care. (The knowledge graph localizes SCD damage to bone/marrow; the splenic mechanism is the established clinical driver behind the pneumonia and bacteremia signals above.)

**2. Impaired opsonization and complement.** SCD patients have defective **alternative-pathway complement activation** and reduced opsonic capacity even beyond splenic loss, further weakening defense against encapsulated organisms.

**3. Chronic hemolysis → free heme/iron.** Intravascular hemolysis releases hemoglobin and free heme, which (a) is a growth substrate favoring iron-avid organisms such as *Salmonella*, and (b) scavenges nitric oxide and drives endothelial dysfunction and inflammation. Transfusion-related iron overload compounds this and is a risk factor for *Yersinia*/*Salmonella*.

**4. Vaso-occlusion and tissue ischemia.** Microvascular sickling causes bone/marrow infarction (**osteomyelitis, classically *Salmonella***), pulmonary infarction predisposing to pneumonia/acute chest syndrome, and renal microinfarction (papillary necrosis, hyposthenuria) that impairs urinary clearance — consistent with the high UTI/pyelonephritis burden observed.

**5. Chronic inflammatory/immune dysregulation.** Persistent hemolysis-driven inflammation, neutrophil and platelet activation, and repeated hospital/device exposure add a background of blunted, dysregulated immunity that raises risk of nosocomial and *S. aureus* infections (MSSA/MRSA and septic shock appear in the cohort).

**Bottom line:** respiratory and urinary infections are the most frequently coded, but the mechanistically defining susceptibility is to **encapsulated bacteria via functional asplenia**, amplified by complement/opsonization defects, hemolysis-derived free heme/iron, and ischemic tissue damage from vaso-occlusion.
