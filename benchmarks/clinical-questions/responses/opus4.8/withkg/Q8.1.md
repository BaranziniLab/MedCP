**No — in this database, postmenopausal women started on anabolic agents had a *higher*, not lower, 180-day fracture-admission rate than those started on oral bisphosphonates.**

| Group (incident users) | Patients | With fracture inpatient admission ≤180 d | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anabolic (teriparatide / abaloparatide / romosozumab) | 1,530 | 66 | **4.31%** |
| Oral bisphosphonate (alendronate / risedronate / ibandronate) | 8,504 | 142 | **1.67%** |

Anabolic initiators had ~2.6× the 180-day fracture-admission rate of bisphosphonate initiators — the opposite of the hypothesized direction.

### How this was defined
- **Population:** women (`gender_concept_id=8532`) with an osteoporosis diagnosis (`Postmenopausal osteoporosis` 4010333, `Osteoporosis` 80502, or the postmenopausal osteoporotic-fracture code 1245005).
- **Exposure:** incident/new use — first-ever drug exposure to each class (RxNorm ingredient + all descendants), used as the index date.
- **Outcome:** an inpatient admission (`visit_concept_id=9201`) with a bone-fracture condition (descendants of `Fracture of bone`, 75053) starting within 180 days of index.

### Important caveat — this is almost certainly confounding by indication
This is a raw, **unadjusted descriptive** comparison, and the higher rate in the anabolic arm is the expected artifact of real-world prescribing: anabolic agents (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab) are reserved for **severe/very-high-risk osteoporosis**, often patients with prior or recent fractures, whereas oral bisphosphonates are first-line for lower-risk patients. So the anabolic group is sicker at baseline.

To make a valid causal comparison you would need to:
- restrict to true new users with a clean baseline (no fracture admission before index),
- adjust for baseline fracture history, age, BMD/FRAX, comorbidities (e.g., propensity-score matching), and
- account for competing exposure switching and follow-up time (person-time rates, not just proportions).
