### Evidence-based answer to the underlying clinical question

Independent of this dataset, the comparative-effectiveness literature on early/aggressive RA answers this fairly directly:

**Which strategy gives fewer flares/lower disease activity?** The landmark head-to-head trials — **TEAR**, **RACAT**, **Swefot**, and **tREACH** — found that **triple conventional therapy (MTX + sulfasalazine + hydroxychloroquine) is largely non-inferior to MTX + a TNF inhibitor** for disease-activity control and radiographic progression at 1–2 years. RACAT explicitly showed non-inferiority of triple therapy to etanercept+MTX.

However, at **early time points (the first ~3–6 months)**, the biologic+MTX arm tends to show **faster onset of response and fewer early flares** — Swefot and tREACH both saw more rapid DAS28 improvement with the biologic strategy before the conventional arm caught up. So for a **90-day** window specifically, **biologic + MTX is the strategy expected to show the lower flare proportion**, with the gap narrowing to roughly equivalent by 6–12 months.

**Immune-pathway differences that plausibly explain the early advantage:**
- **Direct cytokine neutralization vs. broad antimetabolite action.** TNF inhibitors immediately sequester soluble/membrane **TNF-α**, cutting the downstream **NF-κB** signaling that drives synovial IL-1/IL-6, adhesion molecules, osteoclastogenesis (RANKL), and neutrophil recruitment. This produces a rapid drop in synovitis. MTX/SSZ/HCQ act more slowly and indirectly (adenosine-mediated anti-inflammatory signaling, folate-pathway inhibition of lymphocyte proliferation, TLR9/antigen-presentation dampening for HCQ), so their pharmacodynamic onset is weeks-to-months.
- **IL-6 axis** (if tocilizumab is in the biologic arm): blocking IL-6 trans-signaling rapidly reverses acute-phase response (CRP), Th17 differentiation, and systemic flare symptoms.
- **T-cell co-stimulation / B-cell axes** (abatacept, rituximab) act earlier on the ACPA-driven adaptive arm that characterizes seropositive/erosive "aggressive" RA — the exact subgroup most likely to flare on conventional DMARDs alone.
- **Seropositive/ACPA-positive erosive disease** is more TNF- and citrulline-immune-complex-driven, which is mechanistically why the aggressive subgroup benefits most from early targeted cytokine blockade rather than waiting out the slower conventional agents.
