## On the mechanism question (independent of the pending numbers)

The literature (IMPACT, ETHOS, KRONOS, FLAME) is directionally clear and worth stating regardless of what our EHR proportions show:

- **In frequent exacerbators, ICS-inclusive regimens (triple therapy) generally lower the exacerbation rate vs LABA/LAMA — but the benefit is concentrated in patients with high blood eosinophils** (roughly ≥300 cells/µL). Below ~100 cells/µL the benefit largely disappears and pneumonia risk dominates.
- **Immunologic rationale:** eosinophilic COPD exacerbations reflect **type-2 (Th2/ILC2, IL-4/IL-5/IL-13)** airway inflammation, which is corticosteroid-responsive — ICS suppress eosinophil recruitment/survival and type-2 cytokine signaling. **Non-eosinophilic exacerbations** are predominantly **neutrophilic/Th1–Th17 (IL-8, IL-17), often bacterial/viral-driven and relatively corticosteroid-resistant**, and ICS in that setting mainly increases airway infection/pneumonia risk (blunted innate defense) without cutting exacerbations. LABA/LAMA reduce exacerbations chiefly through **bronchodilation and reduced air trapping**, a non-immunologic mechanism.
