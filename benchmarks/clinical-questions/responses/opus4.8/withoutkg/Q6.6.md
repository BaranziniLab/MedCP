## Immune signaling that could underlie the shared inflammation

The EIMs cluster because IBD and its extraintestinal targets share **barrier-tissue innate + Th17/Th1 circuitry**, with gut-primed lymphocytes trafficking to joints, skin, eye, and liver:

- **IL-23 / Th17 axis (IL-17A, IL-22, GM-CSF).** The strongest unifying pathway. IL-23R risk alleles are shared across IBD, ankylosing spondylitis, psoriasis, and uveitis. Gut-primed Th17 cells and IL-23–responsive innate cells at "entheseal" and barrier sites drive the arthropathy–skin–eye triad. Clinically this is why anti–IL-23/IL-12-23 (ustekinumab, risankizumab) and, in the SpA/skin space, anti–IL-17 agents work — though anti–IL-17 can paradoxically worsen gut disease, underscoring tissue-specific IL-17 biology.
- **TNF-α.** Central shared effector; anti-TNF response across IBD, peripheral arthritis, PG, erythema nodosum, and uveitis reflects a common TNF-driven granulomatous/neutrophilic program.
- **Gut→tissue lymphocyte trafficking (α4β7 / MAdCAM-1, and CCR9).** Aberrant homing of mucosal effector lymphocytes to synovium, skin, and hepatobiliary endothelium ("gut–joint" and "gut–liver" axes). PSC is thought to arise from gut-primed T cells recruited to liver via aberrant MAdCAM-1/CCL25 expression on hepatic endothelium.
- **Neutrophil-driven innate inflammation (IL-1β, IL-8/CXCL, IL-36).** Underlies the neutrophilic dermatoses pyoderma gangrenosum and erythema nodosum.
- **Th1/IFN-γ and dysregulated Treg/IL-10.** Contributes to granulomatous Crohn's-type inflammation and loss of tolerance at multiple barriers.
- **Systemic inflammatory / IL-6–hepcidin axis.** Drives the anemia of inflammation (hepcidin blocks iron export → functional iron deficiency) layered on true iron/vit-D deficiency and bone loss — connecting the most prevalent "comorbidities" back to the same cytokine milieu.

**Caveats:** these are cohort prevalences from coded EHR data (concept-based counts, no age/sex standardization or control comparison), so they show relative burden within IBD, not causal risk ratios. The mechanistic pathways are well-supported by genetics and trial data but remain associative at the individual-patient level.
