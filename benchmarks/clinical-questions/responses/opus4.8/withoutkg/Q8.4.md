## How these are biologically related

The pattern is not random comorbidity — it reflects the shared pathophysiology radiating from an atherothrombotic index event:

- **Common upstream driver — systemic atherosclerosis.** Hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes are the *causal* risk substrate. Their re-coding after the event reflects the same endothelial dysfunction / lipid-laden plaque biology that produced the index MI or stroke, now being actively managed. This is why recurrent events in a *different* vascular bed (cerebral infarction after MI, coronary atherosclerosis after stroke) are so prominent: atherosclerosis is a whole-body disease, so an event in one territory marks elevated risk in all.

- **Downstream of myocardial injury — the heart-failure/arrhythmia axis.** Infarcted myocardium loses contractility and undergoes fibrotic remodeling → **congestive heart failure**; the electrically heterogeneous scar and atrial stretch promote **atrial fibrillation** and other arrhythmias (captured as abnormal ECG). AF in turn is cardioembolic, feeding back into recurrent stroke.

- **Cardiorenal and hemodynamic coupling.** Reduced cardiac output plus contrast/nephrotoxic exposure during revascularization drives **acute kidney injury**; the failing heart and kidney share the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone axis, linking heart failure, CKD, hypotension, and **hyponatremia** (neurohormonal water retention).

- **Peri-hospitalization pulmonary and iatrogenic burden.** Pleural effusion, hypoxemia, and dyspnea are largely the pulmonary manifestation of cardiac congestion, while atelectasis and UTI reflect immobilization and instrumentation during the acute admission rather than distinct disease biology.

- **Anemia** sits at the intersection: partly blood loss from antithrombotic therapy and procedures, partly anemia of chronic kidney/heart disease — and it worsens myocardial oxygen supply, closing another feedback loop.

**Bottom line:** the 90-day diagnostic landscape is dominated by (1) the atherosclerotic risk factors that caused the event, (2) recurrent atherothrombosis in adjacent vascular beds, and (3) the heart-failure → arrhythmia → cardiorenal cascade that follows myocardial/cerebral injury — all downstream of one shared vascular-endothelial disease process.

*Caveats:* this counts recorded diagnoses (coding/billing artifacts and surveillance bias inflate risk-factor and "abnormal ECG/lung field" codes), the index definition is limited to acute MI and cerebral infarction, and I did not exclude pre-existing conditions, so much of the hypertension/hyperlipidemia signal is re-documentation of known comorbidity rather than incident disease.
