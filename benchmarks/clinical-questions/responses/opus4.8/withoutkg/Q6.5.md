## Neurotransmitter systems that may link them

The clustering isn't random: the substances that co-occur most sit on the same circuits implicated in bipolar mood regulation.

- **Dopamine (mesolimbic reward)** — the common thread. Manic states involve dopaminergic hyperactivity, and nicotine, alcohol, stimulants (amphetamine/cocaine), and opioids all converge on dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens/VTA. This shared reward-circuit dysregulation is the leading explanation for why the top substances here (nicotine, alcohol, stimulants, cocaine) are exactly the dopaminergic ones. Stimulants can also directly precipitate or mimic manic episodes.
- **GABA / glutamate (excitatory–inhibitory balance)** — alcohol and sedative/hypnotics are GABA-A agonists; withdrawal produces glutamatergic rebound. Bipolar disorder itself shows glutamate/GABA imbalance, and several mood stabilizers (valproate, lamotrigine) act here — a mechanistic overlap that can drive "self-medication" of mixed/agitated states.
- **Endogenous opioid & endocannabinoid systems** — opioid and cannabis use interact with mood, impulsivity, and stress-reward signaling; the endocannabinoid system modulates both dopamine tone and emotional regulation.
- **Serotonin & noradrenaline** — broadly dysregulated in mood disorders and modulated by alcohol and stimulants, contributing to shared impulsivity and affective instability.

**Two non-exclusive frameworks** explain the pattern: *self-medication* (using substances to blunt or lift mood states) and *shared vulnerability* (overlapping genetics and reward-circuit/dopaminergic dysfunction predisposing to both). The strong nicotine and alcohol signals here are consistent with both.
