## Biology that may connect them to airway disease

These co-occur far more than chance, and the mechanisms are bidirectional rather than incidental:

- **Systemic low-grade inflammation ("spill-over").** COPD is characterized by elevated circulating IL-6, TNF-α, and CRP from the diseased lung and from smoking. These same cytokines drive hepatic insulin resistance and dyslipidemia, providing a shared inflammatory axis linking COPD to T2D, hyperlipidemia, and metabolic syndrome.

- **Hypoxia and oxidative stress.** Chronic and intermittent hypoxemia activates HIF pathways and sympathetic tone, worsens insulin resistance, and promotes an atherogenic lipid profile — tying airflow limitation directly to glucose and lipid dysregulation.

- **Physical inactivity and skeletal-muscle dysfunction.** Dyspnea limits activity, reducing insulin-sensitive muscle mass and promoting visceral adiposity and dyslipidemia. Obesity in turn restricts chest-wall mechanics and reduces functional residual capacity, worsening symptoms.

- **Corticosteroid exposure.** Frequent systemic (and to a lesser degree inhaled) glucocorticoids for exacerbations induce hyperglycemia, precipitate/worsen diabetes, and contribute to dyslipidemia and weight gain — an iatrogenic contributor to the metabolic cluster.

- **Adipose tissue as an endocrine organ.** In obesity, adipokine shifts (↑leptin, ↓adiponectin) amplify airway and systemic inflammation, plausibly worsening the COPD phenotype (obese-COPD).

- **Thyroid–respiratory coupling.** Hypothyroidism blunts central respiratory drive and weakens respiratory muscles, aggravating hypercapnia and dyspnea; smoking and chronic illness also perturb the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis, which may partly explain the 20% thyroid burden here.

- **Shared exposure — tobacco.** Smoking is a common upstream cause of both COPD and accelerated atherosclerosis/insulin resistance, so some clustering reflects confounding by a common risk factor rather than direct causation.

"Pulmonary hypertension" is a consequence of COPD itself rather than a metabolic/endocrine comorbidity — included only for context.
