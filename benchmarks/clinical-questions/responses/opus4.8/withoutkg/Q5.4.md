## Answer

**Among adults with newly diagnosed T2DM, the three most commonly started first medications are:**

| Rank | Medication | First-start patients* |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Metformin** | 5,122 |
| 2 | **Glipizide** (sulfonylurea) | 1,002 |
| 3 | **Empagliflozin** (SGLT2 inhibitor) | 490 |

\*Distinct adults (≥18 at diagnosis) whose first A10 antidiabetic *prescription/dispensing* fell within 90 days of their first-ever T2DM diagnosis.

### Why insulin is excluded despite higher raw counts
Insulin products (aspart, glargine, lispro) show higher raw first-drug counts, but this is a well-known EHR artifact:
- A large fraction of first insulin records are **inpatient administration records** (~9,900 of first drugs), i.e. sliding-scale/hospital glycemic control, not deliberate outpatient initiation of chronic therapy.
- Even after limiting to prescriptions/dispensings, short-acting inpatient-type insulins persist because acutely hyperglycemic or hospitalized new diagnoses skew the "first exposure."

Filtering to genuine outpatient oral/injectable **oral-first agents**, the ranking follows the expected clinical pattern: **metformin** as dominant first-line, then a **sulfonylurea (glipizide)**, then an **SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin)**.

### Caveats
- "Newly diagnosed" = first-ever recorded T2DM condition in this database; earlier undocumented history can't be excluded.
- The 90-day window and drug-type filter are analytic choices; widening the window or including administrations shifts insulin up.
- Combination products are attributed to their component ingredient(s).
