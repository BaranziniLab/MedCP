The organ-system pattern is also textbook: thyroid dysfunction, colitis, pneumonitis, arthritis, and hypophysitis dominate, with hypophysitis/adrenal notably enriched in the anti-CTLA-4 arm (15/119 ≈ 13% vs 31/1182 ≈ 3% for anti-PD-1).

## Important caveats before over-reading this

- **Anti-PD-L1 n = 25** — the 44% is on a tiny denominator; that arm's estimate is unstable and the CI would be wide. Atezolizumab/durvalumab/avelumab are not standard first-line melanoma agents, so these are likely off-label or later-line.
- **Class is assigned by *first* ICI only.** Many melanoma patients receive **combination ipi+nivo**; a same-day ipilimumab + nivolumab start is counted here as anti-CTLA-4 (or ties broken arbitrarily), which inflates the anti-CTLA-4 arm's rate since combination therapy is the most toxic regimen of all. A cleaner analysis would model a separate *combination* group.
- **Association, not adjusted.** These are crude rates — no adjustment for age, stage, line of therapy, or follow-up time, and no formal test. Sicker/combination patients self-select into anti-CTLA-4.
- **Prevalent vs incident irAEs.** I required onset *after* the index date, but conditions like hypothyroidism can be pre-existing and re-coded; a true incident analysis would exclude patients with the diagnosis before index.
- **Coding-based outcome.** irAEs are proxied by condition codes (not attributed causally to the drug), so both false positives (background disease) and false negatives (under-coding) exist.

Bottom line: **directionally yes and consistent with known pharmacology, but the difference is modest, unadjusted, and confounded by combination therapy — treat it as hypothesis-generating, not a definitive class comparison.** Want me to (a) break out an explicit ipi+nivo combination group, (b) add exact 95% CIs and a chi-square/Fisher test, or (c) restrict to incident irAEs (no prior diagnosis)?
