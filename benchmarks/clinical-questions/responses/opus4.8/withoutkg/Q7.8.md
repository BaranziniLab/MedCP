| Rank | Drug class (ATC) | Patients exposed | % with abnormal electrolyte ≤30d |
|-----|------------------|-----------------:|-------------------:|
| 1 | **Loop / high-ceiling diuretics** (C03C) | 102,272 | **48.9%** |
| 2 | **Proton-pump inhibitors** (A02BC) | 197,263 | **30.9%** |
| 3 | **K-sparing / aldosterone antagonists** (C03D) | 42,430 | **26.3%** |
| 4 | **Thiazide diuretics** (C03A) | 54,425 | **23.0%** |
| 5 | **ACE inhibitors** (C09AA) | 76,863 | **19.5%** |
| 6 | **ARBs** (C09CA) | 78,359 | **15.2%** |

**Top of the list: loop diuretics** — roughly half of newly-exposed patients had an abnormal electrolyte within 30 days, well above every other class. PPIs and potassium-sparing/aldosterone antagonists follow, then thiazides and the RAAS blockers (ACEi > ARB).

This ordering matches the mechanisms the KG encodes: loop and thiazide diuretics drive K⁺/Na⁺/Mg²⁺ loss, K-sparing agents and ACEi/ARB raise K⁺, and PPIs are the classic hypomagnesemia culprit.

### Method
- **Drug-class layer:** ATC classes known for electrolyte disturbance, mapped ATC → RxNorm ingredient → drug products via `concept_ancestor`, joined to `drug_exposure`.
- **Abnormal electrolyte:** serum Na, K, Cl, Ca, Mg, PO₄ (`measurement`), flagged by standard clinical reference thresholds (stored `range_low/high` were populated in only ~1.4% of rows, so fixed cutoffs were used).
- **Precedence:** per patient, first exposure to the class → any abnormal electrolyte in the 1–30 day window (`DATEADD(day,30,...)`). Denominator is patients exposed; numerator is those with a qualifying abnormal lab.

### Caveats
- **Association, not causation** — these are electrolytes drawn in the 30 days after starting the drug, in patients often sick enough to be monitored (especially loop diuretics in heart-failure/AKI settings); no adjustment for comorbidity, baseline abnormality, or concurrent drugs.
- **Two classes are excluded from the ranking:** glucocorticoids (H02AB, ~529K patients) and macrolides (J01FA) — their cohorts were too large for the per-patient query to finish inside the DB timeout. They're secondary electrolyte offenders; the clinically dominant classes above are complete. I can retry those on a patient sample if you want them included.
