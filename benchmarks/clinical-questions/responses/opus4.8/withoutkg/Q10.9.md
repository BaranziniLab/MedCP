## The trafficking/inflammation biology

- **TNF inhibitors (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab)** neutralize soluble and membrane TNF-α, driving mucosal T-cell apoptosis and shutting down the downstream NF-κB inflammatory cascade *systemically and rapidly*. Onset within days makes them the evidence-based choice for **acute severe UC** (IV infliximab rescue therapy), where you need fast control to avoid colectomy.
- **Vedolizumab** is a gut-selective anti-α4β7 integrin antibody. It blocks α4β7 on gut-homing lymphocytes from binding **MAdCAM-1** on intestinal endothelial venules, preventing lymphocyte trafficking into the gut mucosa without systemic immunosuppression. This gut-selectivity gives a strong safety profile, but the mechanism is *slow* — it works by attrition of mucosal lymphocyte influx over weeks, so it is poorly suited to inducing rapid remission in a hospitalized flare, which is exactly why early steroid rescue is not reduced in the severe subgroup.

**Bottom line:** Mechanistically and in these data, the gut-selective integrin does *not* show lower 60-day steroid rescue in severe UC; TNF inhibitors are the more appropriate rapid-onset agent for acute severe disease, while vedolizumab's α4β7–MAdCAM-1 blockade is a slower, gut-restricted mechanism better suited to maintenance than to acute rescue. Numbers here are observational, unadjusted, and limited by a small integrin arm.
