## Agent's Response

**Answer: Sedative-hypnotic deprescribing** is followed by the lower 90-day fall proportion.

| Deprescribing pattern | Eligible (≥65, ≥2 conditions) | Fell within 90d | Proportion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anticholinergics (ATC N06AA, G04BD, R06A) | 95,571 | 37 | **0.039%** |
| Sedative-hypnotics (N05C/CD/CF, N05BA, N03AE) | 268,313 | 58 | **0.022%** |

Risk ratio ≈ 1.79 favoring sedative-hypnotic withdrawal (two-proportion z ≈ 2.4, p ≈ 0.016).

**Why, neurochemically:**
- **Sedative-hypnotics** (benzodiazepines, Z-drugs) are GABA-A positive allosteric modulators. Their fall risk is a *direct, ongoing* pharmacodynamic effect — sedation, psychomotor slowing, blunted postural reflexes, next-day residual hypnosis. Stop the drug and the hazard clears on the timescale of drug elimination, so the 90-day window shows the benefit promptly.
- **Anticholinergics** cause harm through muscarinic and central cholinergic blockade — gait/attention deficits, orthostatic hypotension, blurred vision, delirium. These accrue with chronic exposure and reverse *slowly*; abrupt withdrawal can also provoke transient **cholinergic rebound** (agitation, dizziness). The underlying deficit doesn't fully resolve inside 90 days, leaving residual near-term fall risk.

**Important caveats:** these are crude, unadjusted event proportions. The two classes differ sharply in confounding-by-indication and channelling (e.g., sedative-hypnotics may be deprescribed in more robust patients; anticholinergic bladder/antidepressant users carry different baseline frailty). Falls are very sparsely coded in this database (~4,700 total events), the multimorbidity criterion is a ≥2 condition-era proxy, and there's no death/competing-risk handling. Treat this as **hypothesis-generating**, not causal — a matched or IPTW-adjusted analysis with a fall-risk covariate set would be the next step.
