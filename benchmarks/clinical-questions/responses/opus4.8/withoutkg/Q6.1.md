## Plausible biological underpinnings

**Female-predominant patterns**
- **Osteoporosis / osteoarthritis / bone disorders (largest gap):** post-menopausal estrogen loss accelerates bone resorption; estrogen also modulates cartilage and joint tissue. Reduced estrogen after menopause is the dominant mechanism, compounded by lower peak bone mass and longer survival into old age in women.
- **UTI:** shorter female urethra and peri-urethral colonization, plus post-menopausal urogenital atrophy (loss of estrogen-dependent vaginal *Lactobacillus* and mucosal defense) raise ascending-infection risk — amplified in dementia by incontinence and reduced fluid intake.
- **Hypothyroidism:** autoimmune (Hashimoto) thyroid disease is several-fold more common in women, an X-linked/sex-hormone-influenced autoimmune predisposition; overlap of hypothyroid cognitive slowing with dementia may also increase ascertainment.
- **Depression:** consistent female preponderance across life, tied to HPA-axis and gonadal-hormone reactivity plus differential reporting/ascertainment.

**Male-predominant patterns**
- **Coronary atherosclerosis, arrhythmia, AF, CKD/AKI:** men develop atherosclerotic vascular disease earlier (women are relatively protected pre-menopause by estrogen's favorable lipid/endothelial effects). This vascular burden plausibly reflects a **larger vascular contribution to their dementia** — consistent with the higher rates of coronary disease and renal vascular injury, and pointing toward vascular/mixed rather than purely neurodegenerative etiology in men.
- **Pneumonia/sepsis, hypotension:** may reflect later-stage or more medically fragile presentation and sex differences in immune response.

## Important caveats
- These are **observational EHR prevalences, not adjusted rates.** Women in the cohort are more numerous and typically older at dementia diagnosis (they live longer), so age confounds several differences (osteoporosis, UTI especially).
- Many concepts are **symptoms/findings** ("chest pain," "abnormal ECG," "impaired cognition"), not clean diagnoses — reflecting documentation, not just disease.
- Diagnostic **ascertainment differs by sex** (e.g., women screened more for osteoporosis/thyroid), inflating some gaps.
- Dementia subtype is not separated here; the male vascular-comorbidity signal would be worth confirming by stratifying Alzheimer vs. vascular dementia.
