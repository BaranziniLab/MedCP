In this OMOP EHR dataset, initial carbapenem therapy for gram-negative UTI was **not associated with reduced 60-day recurrence** — if anything, recurrence was numerically *higher* in the carbapenem arm.

| Initial therapy | N eligible | Culture-confirmed recurrence (15–60d) | Any UTI recurrence (15–60d) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbapenem | 406 | 46 (11.3%) | 126 (31.0%) |
| Narrow-spectrum | 2,670 | 211 (7.9%) | 706 (26.4%) |
| **Risk ratio (carbapenem vs narrow)** | | **1.43** (95% CI ~1.06–1.94) | **1.17** (95% CI ~1.01–1.36) |

**Cohort definition:** first gram-negative urine culture (E. coli, Klebsiella, Proteus, Pseudomonas, Enterobacter, Citrobacter, Morganella, Serratia, or explicitly "gram-negative"; concept `3026008`) as the index event; initial therapy = first carbapenem (meropenem/imipenem/ertapenem/doripenem) vs narrow-spectrum agent (nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim/TMP-SMX, fosfomycin, cephalexin, cefazolin) within 0–3 days; recurrence = new UTI diagnosis or new gram-negative urine culture on days 15–60; all patients required to be under observation through day 60.

**Interpretation — this is confounding by indication, not benefit reversal.** The signal almost certainly reflects that carbapenems are reserved for sicker patients and resistant/ESBL organisms who are inherently more prone to recurrence. This is unadjusted observational data and cannot support a causal claim.

**Major caveats:**
- **No confounder adjustment** (age, comorbidity, prior UTIs, catheter, complicated vs uncomplicated, ESBL/resistance status, care setting). The arms are not exchangeable.
- **Organism identification is free-text** (`value_source_value`), not coded — susceptible to parsing miss/misclassification, and doesn't distinguish colonization/contamination from true infection.
- **Narrow-spectrum agents (esp. nitrofurantoin/fosfomycin) are used for uncomplicated lower UTI**, while carbapenems mark complicated/upper-tract/resistant disease — a large baseline severity imbalance.
- Recurrence captured only within one observation period; the 15-day floor was used to exclude same-episode treatment noise.

**Bottom line:** The data provide **no evidence that initial carbapenems reduce 60-day recurrence** in gram-negative UTI, and the crude association runs the opposite direction — consistent with confounding by indication. A defensible conclusion would require propensity-score or resistance-stratified adjustment, which this crude query does not perform. Want me to add covariate adjustment (e.g., age, Charlson, prior-UTI count) or restrict to E. coli / uncomplicated cystitis to reduce the indication bias?
