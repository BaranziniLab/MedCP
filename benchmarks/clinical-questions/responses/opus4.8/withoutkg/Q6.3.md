## Shared biological pathways

The top co-occurring conditions are not independent — they cluster into a few mechanistic groups.

**1. Insulin resistance / metabolic syndrome** — links hyperlipidemia, T2DM, obesity, OSA, and HTN itself.
- Hyperinsulinemia drives renal sodium retention and sympathetic activation → raises BP.
- Adipose tissue is endocrine: excess leptin/reduced adiponectin and free fatty acids promote both dyslipidemia and vascular insulin resistance.
- This is the single strongest cluster — obesity → insulin resistance is the common upstream node for four of the ten comorbidities.

**2. RAAS (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone) overactivity** — links HTN, CKD, and heart failure.
- Angiotensin II raises BP, drives glomerular hyperfiltration/fibrosis (CKD), and induces cardiac hypertrophy and remodeling (HF). CKD in turn worsens volume/sodium handling — a bidirectional loop.

**3. Endothelial dysfunction & chronic inflammation / oxidative stress** — links HTN, atherosclerosis (IHD), CKD, and diabetes.
- Reduced nitric-oxide bioavailability plus elevated ROS, NF-κB signaling and inflammatory cytokines accelerate atherosclerotic plaque and microvascular injury shared across all four.

**4. Sympathetic nervous system overactivity** — links OSA, HTN, atrial fibrillation, and heart failure.
- Intermittent nocturnal hypoxia (OSA) and neurohormonal activation raise catecholamine tone, promoting atrial remodeling/AF and pressure load.

**5. Endocrine/metabolic modulation** — hypothyroidism raises diastolic BP and LDL (impaired lipid clearance), and depression is linked via HPA-axis and sympathetic dysregulation plus shared inflammatory signaling.
