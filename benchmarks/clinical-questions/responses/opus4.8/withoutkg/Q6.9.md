## Plausible appetite/stress pathways linking them

These are mechanistic hypotheses from the literature, not derived from the EHR:

- **HPA axis / cortisol (the strongest bridge).** Chronic anxiety, depression, and trauma dysregulate the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis → sustained cortisol → visceral adiposity, insulin resistance, and increased intake of energy-dense "comfort" food. This is the most parsimonious link for the internalizing cluster above.
- **Reward/dopamine & emotional eating.** Depression and trauma alter mesolimbic dopamine reward signaling; hyperpalatable food becomes a self-soothing behavior. Overlaps with ADHD, where impaired dopaminergic inhibitory control drives impulsive/dysregulated eating.
- **Leptin–insulin CNS resistance.** Obesity-related leptin/insulin resistance impairs hypothalamic satiety and also affects hippocampal/limbic circuits implicated in mood — a bidirectional loop.
- **Serotonin & the gut–brain axis.** Serotonergic tone governs both satiety and mood; diet-driven microbiome shifts and low-grade inflammation feed back on both.
- **Neuroinflammation.** Adipose-derived cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, CRP) cross into the CNS and are associated with depressive symptoms — a shared inflammatory substrate.
- **Sleep disruption.** Obesity (OSA) and anxiety/depression both fragment sleep, dysregulating ghrelin/leptin (↑hunger) and worsening mood — a reinforcing triangle.
- **Medication iatrogenesis.** Some psychotropics used for these very diagnoses (certain antipsychotics, some antidepressants) drive appetite and weight gain, confounding the association.
