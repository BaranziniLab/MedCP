- The anti-IgE group showed **no net suppression** — most patients were flat or rose, and the group started from a much lower baseline (omalizumab is dosed on IgE/weight, not eosinophilia).

The direction, however, matches the pivotal-trial literature exactly (mepolizumab/benralizumab drive blood eosinophils down ~70–100%; omalizumab has little effect on circulating eosinophils).

## How the mechanisms differ

- **IL-5 / IL-5R axis (mepolizumab, reslizumab, benralizumab):** IL-5 is the principal cytokine for eosinophil maturation, survival, and release from marrow.
  - *Mepolizumab, reslizumab* — anti–IL-5 monoclonal antibodies that bind the free cytokine, starving eosinophils of their survival signal → gradual depletion.
  - *Benralizumab* — binds **IL-5Rα** on the eosinophil surface and, being afucosylated, recruits NK cells to kill them by antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity → near-complete, rapid depletion.
  - Net effect: direct, on-target removal of the eosinophil compartment.

- **Anti-IgE (omalizumab):** binds free IgE and blocks its attachment to FcεRI on mast cells/basophils, interrupting the allergic cascade *upstream* of eosinophil recruitment. It reduces exacerbations in allergic (IgE-driven) asthma but does **not** directly target eosinophil biology, so circulating eosinophil counts change little — consistent with the flat/rising values above.

This is the clinical basis for phenotype-directed selection: **eosinophilic** severe asthma → anti–IL-5/IL-5R; **allergic/IgE-high** severe asthma → anti-IgE.
