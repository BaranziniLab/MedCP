This part I can answer from established pharmacology — treat it as hypothesis, to be confirmed against the numbers above:

- **Target/pathway.** LMWHs act indirectly via antithrombin, accelerating inhibition of **factor Xa** (and some IIa). The oral DOACs split into two mechanisms:
  - **Direct factor Xa inhibitors** — apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban.
  - **Direct thrombin (factor IIa) inhibitor** — dabigatran (absent here).
- **Why bleeding risk differs among the Xa inhibitors** despite a shared target:
  - **Dosing schedule / peak-trough.** Apixaban is **twice-daily**, giving lower peak plasma concentrations and flatter exposure; rivaroxaban is **once-daily** with higher peaks — higher Cmax is associated with more mucosal/GI bleeding.
  - **GI luminal exposure.** Rivaroxaban has lower, more variable oral bioavailability with more active drug transiting/absorbed distally, correlating with higher **GI bleeding** signals; apixaban has less local GI anticoagulant effect.
  - **Renal clearance.** Rivaroxaban ~35% renal, edoxaban ~50%, apixaban ~27% — in cancer patients with fluctuating renal function and nephrotoxic chemo, higher renal dependence raises accumulation/bleeding risk.
  - **P-gp / CYP3A4 interactions.** All three are **P-glycoprotein** substrates; apixaban and rivaroxaban are also **CYP3A4** substrates (edoxaban minimally). Azole antifungals, some tyrosine-kinase inhibitors, and other oncology drugs inhibit these pathways and raise DOAC levels.

The consistent prediction is therefore **fewer 90-day bleeding admissions on apixaban** than on rivaroxaban, driven mainly by twice-daily flatter pharmacokinetics and lower GI luminal exposure.
