### The mechanistic / pathway answer (does not need the DB)
This part I can give you now, and it frames what the EHR comparison is likely to show:

- **Apixaban** and **rivaroxaban** both inhibit **Factor Xa**; **dabigatran** inhibits **thrombin (Factor IIa)**; **edoxaban** is also a Factor Xa inhibitor.
- **Where bleeding risk in cancer patients diverges — the GI mucosa.** Rivaroxaban and edoxaban carry consistently **higher GI/major bleeding signals in cancer-associated thrombosis** (SELECT-D, Hokusai-VTE Cancer), whereas apixaban showed **comparable or lower major bleeding** vs LMWH in the Caravaggio and ADAM-VTE trials. The plausible mechanisms:
  - **Luminal drug exposure / bioavailability.** Rivaroxaban is dosed once-daily at higher peak concentrations and a substantial unabsorbed fraction transits the GI tract; edoxaban has ~62% oral bioavailability with meaningful luminal exposure. Apixaban's twice-daily, lower-peak profile and lower fractional luminal load reduce topical anticoagulant effect on a **tumor-bearing, friable GI mucosa** — the dominant bleeding site in GI and luminal cancers.
  - **Factor Xa vs thrombin.** Thrombin (dabigatran's target) also drives platelet activation (PAR-1/PAR-4) and fibrin cross-linking; Xa inhibitors leave thrombin-mediated primary hemostasis at existing clots relatively more intact, which may matter at friable tumor surfaces — but dabigatran's own dyspepsia/GI-irritant profile offsets this and it is not first-line in CAT.
  - **P-gp / CYP3A4 interactions.** Many oncology regimens (e.g., certain TKIs, azoles, dexamethasone) modulate **P-glycoprotein and CYP3A4**, altering DOAC plasma levels — rivaroxaban and apixaban are both CYP3A4/P-gp substrates, edoxaban and dabigatran are mainly P-gp. Concomitant strong inhibitors raise levels and bleeding; inducers raise recurrent-VTE risk. This interaction burden is a key confounder the EHR analysis should adjust for.
