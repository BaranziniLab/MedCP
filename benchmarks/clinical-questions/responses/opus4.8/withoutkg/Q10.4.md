## Caveat on magnitude

This is a small, statistically fragile gap (a two-proportion test on these counts gives p ≈ 0.7 — not significant). Both arms sit near 70%, which is high and reflects that this is a sick post-MI/HFrEF cohort with frequent rehospitalization; it is an unadjusted, observational proportion, not a causal effect. Confounding by indication is very likely — carvedilol (a "less selective" α/β-blocker) is preferentially given to sicker HFrEF patients, which would bias its arm toward more readmission. Treat the direction as suggestive only.

## Receptor signaling rationale for a plausible β1-selective advantage

- **β1 is the therapeutic target in the failing heart.** Chronic post-MI catecholamine excess drives β1-mediated cAMP→PKA overactivity, Ca²⁺ overload, arrhythmia, apoptosis, and adverse remodeling. Selective β1 blockade blunts exactly this axis while sparing other receptors.
- **Sparing β2** preserves peripheral/bronchial vasodilation and reduces bronchospasm and metabolic side effects, improving tolerability and adherence — a real driver of readmission in this population.
- **β1 receptor downregulation/uncoupling** in HFrEF means β1-selective agents concentrate blockade on the receptor subtype that is pathologically overstimulated, potentially yielding cleaner rate/remodeling benefit per unit of blockade.
- **Counterpoint:** guideline-preferred HFrEF agents are actually bisoprolol and metoprolol succinate (β1-selective) *and* carvedilol (non-selective, with α1-blockade and antioxidant effects) — carvedilol's α1 blockade lowers afterload and its broader profile is mortality-proven. So mechanism does not cleanly favor selectivity; the near-identical rates here are consistent with both classes being effective.

**Bottom line:** β1-selective agents had the marginally lower 90-day readmission proportion, but the difference is within noise and confounded — best read as "no meaningful class difference in this cohort," with β1-selectivity offering a mechanistically clean but not decisively superior rationale.
