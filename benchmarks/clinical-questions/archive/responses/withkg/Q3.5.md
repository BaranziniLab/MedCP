**Potassium Level Distribution:**

- **Mean in hypokalemic patients:** 3.13 mmol/L  
- **Mean in normal patients:** 4.21 mmol/L
- **Range:** 0.7 - 22.1 mmol/L (some extreme values likely represent measurement errors)

### **Clinical Implications:**
1. **High Risk:** Nearly half of new loop diuretic users develop hypokalemia within 30 days
2. **Monitoring Gap:** ~37% of patients lacked potassium monitoring within 30 days
3. **Clinical Significance:** This finding supports guidelines recommending routine electrolyte monitoring in patients starting loop diuretics

### **Limitations:**
- Analysis limited to patients with laboratory monitoring
- Some extreme values suggest potential data quality issues
- Definition of "new user" based on first recorded prescription in database
- 30-day window may miss some cases of delayed hypokalemia

**Recommendation:** Healthcare providers should ensure systematic potassium monitoring for all patients starting loop diuretics, given the high incidence of hypokalemia observed in this real-world data.
