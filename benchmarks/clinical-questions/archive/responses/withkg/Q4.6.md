- **Dramatic Spring Peak**: April and May show the highest steroid burst rates (averages of 4,409 and 8,748 per month respectively) - this is particularly striking for May
- **Secondary Fall Peak**: November shows elevated steroid prescriptions (average 2,831 per month)
- **Summer patterns**: Mixed results with some elevation in June-July

### **Clinical Interpretation:**

**Yes, both metrics do show fall and spring peaks, but with important nuances:**

1. **Spring Peak (March-May)**: Very pronounced, especially for steroid bursts. This aligns with increased pollen exposure and allergen activity during spring months.

2. **Fall Peak (September-November)**: Present but less dramatic than spring. This corresponds to back-to-school viral exposures, ragweed season, and environmental changes.

3. **The steroid burst pattern is more dramatic than ED visits**, suggesting that many exacerbations are managed in outpatient settings with oral corticosteroids rather than requiring emergency care.

### **Clinical Significance:**
- The data confirms known seasonal patterns in pediatric asthma exacerbations
- Spring allergen season (particularly May) represents the highest risk period
- Fall represents a secondary peak, likely related to viral respiratory infections and late-season allergens
