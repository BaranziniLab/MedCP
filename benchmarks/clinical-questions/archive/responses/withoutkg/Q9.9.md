```sql
-- User cohort identification
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT de.person_id) as total_users
FROM omop.drug_exposure de

-- OR progestin-only criteria

-- VTE event analysis

FROM omop.condition_occurrence co, omop.drug_exposure de, omop.concept c, omop.concept c2

WHERE c.concept_id = 318736  -- Migraine concept
```

- **Combined Oral Contraceptives**: 5,652 users
- **Progestin-only Methods**: 12,355 users
- **Study Period**: 2020-2023

### **12-Month Event Rates:**

#### **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):**
- **Combined Oral Contraceptives**: 17 events
  - **Rate**: 3.01 per 1,000 users per year
- **Progestin-only Methods**: 100 events  
  - **Rate**: 8.09 per 1,000 users per year

#### **Migraine:**
- **Combined Oral Contraceptives**: 567 events
  - **Rate**: 100.3 per 1,000 users per year
- **Progestin-only Methods**: 1,287 events
  - **Rate**: 104.2 per 1,000 users per year

### **Key Findings:**

1. **VTE Risk**: Surprisingly, this database showed a **lower** VTE rate in combined OC users (3.01/1000) compared to progestin-only users (8.09/1000). This finding is **contrary to established clinical literature** which shows combined OCs carry higher VTE risk.

2. **Migraine Rates**: Very similar between groups - combined OCs (100.3/1000) vs progestin-only (104.2/1000).

### **Important Limitations:**

⚠️ **Critical Caveats:**
- These results **contradict well-established clinical evidence** that combined OCs increase VTE risk 3-6 fold compared to non-use
- The database may have **selection bias** - women at higher VTE risk may preferentially be prescribed progestin-only methods
- **Temporal relationships** between contraceptive initiation and events need further validation
- **Confounding factors** (age, BMI, family history, smoking) not controlled for in this analysis

### **Clinical Context:**
Established literature shows:
- Combined OCs: VTE risk 3-6 times higher than baseline
- Progestin-only methods: Minimal VTE risk increase
- Both can trigger or worsen migraines in susceptible individuals

**Recommendation**: These database findings should **not** change clinical practice. The established evidence base for contraceptive-associated risks remains the gold standard for clinical decision-making. This analysis may reflect database-specific patterns rather than true population risk differences.
