## **Results: Top 3 First-Line Diabetes Medications**

1. **Insulin (13.07% of patients - 21,030 patients)**
   - Various formulations including insulin aspart, insulin glargine, regular insulin
   - Surprisingly high prevalence suggests many patients present with more severe hyperglycemia requiring immediate insulin therapy

2. **Metformin (5.08% of patients - 8,167 patients)**
   - Primarily metformin hydrochloride 500mg and 1000mg tablets
   - Expected as first-line therapy per clinical guidelines due to efficacy, safety profile, and cardiovascular benefits

3. **Glipizide (1.10% of patients - 1,772 patients)**
   - Sulfonylurea class medication
   - Common doses: 5mg and 10mg oral tablets
   - Often used when metformin is contraindicated or as add-on therapy

## **Additional Notable Medications:**
- **Sitagliptin (DPP-4 inhibitor)**: 0.69% of patients
- **Empagliflozin (SGLT-2 inhibitor)**: 0.50% of patients
- **Linagliptin (DPP-4 inhibitor)**: 0.38% of patients

## **Clinical Interpretation:**

The surprisingly high rate of **insulin as first-line therapy (13.07%)** suggests that many patients in this database presented with:
- Severe hyperglycemia (HbA1c >10% or glucose >300 mg/dL)
- Diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state
- Contraindications to oral medications
- Hospitalization at diagnosis requiring immediate glycemic control

**Metformin's lower-than-expected prevalence (5.08%)** may reflect:
- Real-world prescribing patterns differing from guidelines
- Patient comorbidities (kidney disease, heart failure) limiting metformin use
- Healthcare system factors
- Potential under-capture of outpatient oral medications vs. inpatient insulin prescriptions

- The 90-day window may miss some delayed medication starts
- Prescribing patterns may vary by healthcare setting, provider specialty, and patient population
- Always consult current clinical guidelines and consider individual patient factors for treatment decisions
