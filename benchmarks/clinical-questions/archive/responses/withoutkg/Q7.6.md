## **Most Frequently Used Pathway-Defined Drug Classes in RA:**

### **1. Corticosteroids (13,423 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: Anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive via glucocorticoid receptor pathway
- **Most common**: Prednisone (multiple doses), methylprednisolone, prednisolone
- **Pathway**: Suppression of inflammatory gene transcription, inhibition of phospholipase A2

### **2. Folate Antagonists - Methotrexate (3,614 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: Dihydrofolate reductase inhibition, purine/pyrimidine synthesis disruption
- **Pathway**: Interferes with DNA synthesis and cell proliferation, particularly in activated T cells

### **3. Antimalarial DMARDs - Hydroxychloroquine (2,069 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: Interferes with antigen presentation and toll-like receptor signaling
- **Pathway**: Modulates innate immunity, reduces inflammatory cytokine production

### **4. TNF-α Inhibitors (3,799 total patients)**
- **Etanercept** (1,468 patients): TNF receptor fusion protein
- **Adalimumab** (1,421 patients): Anti-TNF monoclonal antibody
- **Infliximab** (502 patients): Chimeric anti-TNF antibody
- **Certolizumab** (220 patients) & **Golimumab** (188 patients)
- **Pathway**: Direct TNF-α neutralization, blocking inflammatory cascade

### **5. Sulfonamide DMARDs - Sulfasalazine (788 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: 5-aminosalicylic acid release, anti-inflammatory effects
- **Pathway**: Inhibits NF-κB activation, reduces inflammatory mediators

### **6. Pyrimidine Synthesis Inhibitors - Leflunomide (748 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase inhibition
- **Pathway**: Blocks de novo pyrimidine synthesis, particularly affecting activated lymphocytes

### **7. JAK Inhibitors (605 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: Janus kinase pathway inhibition
- **Pathway**: Blocks JAK-STAT signaling for multiple cytokines (IL-6, interferons, etc.)

### **8. IL-6 Receptor Antagonists (431 patients)**
- **Drugs**: Tocilizumab, Sarilumab
- **Pathway**: Blocks IL-6-mediated inflammatory signaling

### **9. CTLA-4 Agonists - Abatacept (425 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: T-cell costimulation blockade
- **Pathway**: Prevents full T-cell activation by blocking CD28-B7 interaction

### **10. CD20 Antagonists - Rituximab (411 patients)**
- **Mechanism**: B-cell depletion
- **Pathway**: Targets CD20 on B cells, leading to B-cell lysis and autoantibody reduction

## **Key Findings:**
- **Corticosteroids** are by far the most commonly used, reflecting their role in acute flare management
- **Conventional DMARDs** (methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, leflunomide) represent the backbone of RA treatment
- **TNF inhibitors** are the most frequently used biologics, with etanercept and adalimumab being most common
- **Newer pathways** like JAK inhibitors show growing but still limited usage

**Clinical Note**: These findings reflect real-world prescribing patterns and should be interpreted alongside current RA treatment guidelines. Always consult healthcare professionals for treatment decisions.
