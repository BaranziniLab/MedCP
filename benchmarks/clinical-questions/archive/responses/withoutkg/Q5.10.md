## Key Clinical Insights:

**Most Prevalent Deficiencies:**
1. **Vitamin D deficiency** - by far the most common
2. **Iron deficiency** (with or without anemia) 
3. **Protein-calorie malnutrition**
4. **Vitamin B12 deficiency**

**Age Patterns:**
- Most diagnoses occur between ages 65-90
- B12 deficiency tends to be diagnosed later (average 78.7 years)
- Mild malnutrition is diagnosed at the highest average age (80.4 years)

**Clinical Considerations:**
- **Vitamin D deficiency** is extremely prevalent in elderly populations
- **Iron deficiency** is often related to blood loss (GI bleeding, etc.)
- **Protein-calorie malnutrition** is significant, particularly in institutionalized elderly
- **B12 deficiency** may be underdiagnosed due to subtle symptoms

**Limitations:** This analysis is based on coded diagnoses in EHRs and may underrepresent subclinical deficiencies. Clinical assessment and laboratory testing may reveal higher prevalence rates. Healthcare providers should consider screening for these common deficiencies in elderly patients, particularly vitamin D and iron status.
