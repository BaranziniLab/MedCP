## **Candidate Mechanisms**

### **Nintedanib (Higher Adverse Event Rates):**

1. **Multi-target Kinase Inhibition:**
   - Inhibits VEGFR, FGFR, and PDGFR pathways
   - More extensive off-target effects affecting:
     - **GI tract**: VEGF inhibition disrupts intestinal barrier function
     - **Hepatocytes**: Multiple kinase pathways affect drug metabolism

2. **Hepatotoxicity Mechanism:**
   - **CYP3A4 metabolism** → reactive metabolites
   - **Mitochondrial dysfunction** in hepatocytes
   - **Oxidative stress** from tyrosine kinase inhibition

3. **GI Toxicity Mechanism:**
   - **VEGFR inhibition** → impaired intestinal angiogenesis
   - **Disrupted tight junctions** → increased permeability
   - **Altered gut microbiome** → inflammatory response

### **Pirfenidone (Lower Adverse Event Rates):**

1. **More Selective Anti-fibrotic Action:**
   - Primarily inhibits **TGF-β** and **PDGF** signaling
   - **Less off-target kinase inhibition**
   - More targeted mechanism with fewer systemic effects

2. **Different Metabolic Profile:**
   - **CYP1A2-mediated metabolism** (vs CYP3A4 for nintedanib)
   - **Less reactive metabolite formation**
   - Better hepatic tolerance profile

### **Clinical Implications:**
- **Nintedanib**: Requires more frequent monitoring of liver function and GI symptoms
- **Pirfenidone**: Generally better tolerated but still requires monitoring
- **Patient selection** should consider baseline liver function and GI comorbidities
