### SQL Queries Used:
```sql
-- Main query for H. pylori test frequency
SELECT TOP 20
    c.concept_name,
    c.concept_id,
    COUNT(*) as test_count
FROM omop.measurement m
JOIN omop.concept c ON m.measurement_concept_id = c.concept_id
WHERE (LOWER(c.concept_name) LIKE '%helicobacter%' 
   OR LOWER(c.concept_name) LIKE '%h. pylori%' 
   OR LOWER(c.concept_name) LIKE '%h pylori%'
   OR LOWER(c.concept_name) LIKE '%pylori%')
   AND c.standard_concept = 'S'
   AND c.invalid_reason IS NULL
   AND c.domain_id = 'Measurement'
GROUP BY c.concept_name, c.concept_id
ORDER BY test_count DESC
```

These results align with clinical practice patterns where:

2. **Stool antigen testing** is preferred for confirming eradication after treatment as it detects active infection
3. **Urea breath testing** is considered the gold standard for active infection detection but is less frequently used, likely due to cost and complexity

**Note:** This analysis is based on your EHR data patterns. For clinical decision-making, current guidelines recommend stool antigen testing or urea breath testing over serology for diagnosing active H. pylori infection, as serology can remain positive even after successful eradication.
