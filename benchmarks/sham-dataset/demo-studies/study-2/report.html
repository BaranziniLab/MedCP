1. Executive summary

Source study. Original study on Synthetic Dataset (no published paper is being replicated).

Question. In a critical-care cohort, is a higher blood lactate associated with in-hospital death? This is framed as an association study, not a prediction model.

83patients with ≥1 blood lactate (of 100 in the demo) EHR
15in-hospital deaths (18.1%); all 15 fall inside the lactate cohort EHR
749numeric lactate results, 0.5–13.2 mmol/L EHR
0.68ROC AUC of peak lactate for death (95% CI 0.52–0.83) EHR

Headline result EHR. Peak lactate was higher in non-survivors than survivors (median 3.2 vs 2.8 mmol/L; Hodges–Lehmann difference +1.1 mmol/L, 95% CI 0.1–3.9; Mann–Whitney p=0.031). Peak lactate discriminated death with AUC 0.68 (95% CI 0.52–0.83). Kaplan–Meier survival fell monotonically across peak-lactate tertiles at the 28-day landmark (T1 0.90, T2 0.96, T3 0.77), but the log-rank test was not significant (28-day landmark χ²=3.12, p=0.21; full follow-up p=0.67). Last lactate and lactate clearance did not differ by outcome (p=0.29, p=0.99).

Bottom line. The direction and magnitude are consistent with the well-established lactate–mortality association: elevated peak lactate marks higher in-hospital mortality risk. With only 15 deaths the study is underpowered for time-to-event inference (minimum detectable Cohen's d ≈ 0.80; minimum detectable AUC ≈ 0.73), so the non-significant log-rank is uninformative, not a refutation. No multivariate model was fitted (see §5). SPOKE supplied the biology the EHR cannot: the pyruvate/mitochondrial gene programme behind hyperlactatemia and the therapeutic landscape (antibiotics for the septic source; pyruvate-dehydrogenase–directed lactate-lowering agents).

2.6 Event counts, person-time & minimum detectable effect EHR

  • Events: 15 deaths (non-survivor arm) vs 68 survivors. Deaths per tertile: T1 3, T2 5, T3 7.
  • Person-time (in-hospital, index stay): total 791 patient-days; index-stay length median 7.3 d (IQR 4.8–11.5).
  • Person-time (full follow-up, first lactate → last hospital contact): 30,293 patient-days (deaths 4,347; survivors 25,946). This mixes in- and between-admission time (see §5).
  • Minimum detectable effect at 80% power (α=0.05, two-sided), given 15 vs 68: minimum detectable standardized difference Cohen's d ≈ 0.80 (a large effect); minimum detectable AUC ≈ 0.73 above the null 0.5. The observed AUC (0.68) sits below this threshold, which is exactly why the time-to-event tests do not reach significance. This is a property of the design, reported irrespective of the estimate.

No multivariate model was fitted. With only 15 events, even the ~10-events-per-variable rule of thumb permits at most one covariate; any logistic or Cox model with age, sex, severity, etc. would be overfit and its estimates unreliable. We therefore report descriptive/univariate statistics only, as specified.

3. Figures

4. Knowledge-graph contribution (what SPOKE added)

The EHR returns lactate numbers and a death flag; it explains neither the biology of the elevation nor the therapeutics. SPOKE (traversed live via MedCP) supplied both. Every statement here is knowledge-graph–derived SPOKE.

4.1 Mechanism — why lactate rises SPOKE

The physiological state behind an elevated blood lactate is lactic acidosis (DOID:3650), which SPOKE links to 188 genes via ASSOCIATES_DaG. The gene set is dominated by the machinery of aerobic pyruvate oxidation and mitochondrial ATP production — exactly the pathway that fails in shock/hypoperfusion, forcing pyruvate→lactate:

  • Pyruvate dehydrogenase complex — PDHA1, PDHB, DLD, DLAT (the gate from pyruvate into the TCA cycle; when it stalls, lactate accumulates).
  • Pyruvate handling — PC (pyruvate carboxylase), MPC1 (mitochondrial pyruvate carrier).
  • Oxidative phosphorylation — cytochrome-c-oxidase / complex components (COX1/2/3, COX10, COX15, CYC1, CYTB) and ATP-synthase subunits (ATP5F1A/D/E).
  • Gluconeogenesis / glycogen — FBP1, G6PC1, ALDOB.

By contrast, the specific node bacterial sepsis (DOID:0040085) carries only 1 associated gene (CTNNAL1) in this SPOKE build — a data-sparsity note, not biology (see §5).

4.2 Therapeutics the EHR lactate value cannot suggest SPOKE

(a) Treat the septic source. 42 compounds TREATS_CtD bacterial sepsis — an antibiotic wall spanning cephalosporins (9), aminoglycosides (8), penicillins (2), nitroimidazoles (3), plus glycopeptide/lipoglycopeptide/lipopeptide agents and a lincosamide. Named examples: Vancomycin, Piperacillin, Ceftriaxone, Cefepime, Imipenem, Metronidazole, Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Amikacin, Daptomycin, Clindamycin. Source control lowers the driver of hyperlactatemia.

(b) Lower the lactate directly. 5 compounds TREATS_CtD lactic acidosis, and they map cleanly onto the mechanism in §4.1: Dichloroacetic acid (activates pyruvate dehydrogenase → drives pyruvate into oxidation and lowers lactate), and the mitochondrial cofactors Thiamine (B1, PDH cofactor — thiamine deficiency itself causes lactic acidosis) and Riboflavin (B2). The list also flags Stavudine and Abacavir — NRTIs that cause mitochondrial lactic acidosis, a drug-safety signal the EHR lab value alone would never surface.

Sepsis symptom edges (PRESENTS_DpS) returned empty for DOID:0040085 in this build, so the clinical-picture layer could not be added from SPOKE.

Kept separate (EHR-measured physiology) EHR: the cohort (n=83), all lactate values and their peak/last/clearance derivations, the 15 in-hospital deaths, and every statistic in §2–§3. SPOKE contributed no patient-level data and none of the effect estimates — only mechanism and therapeutic context.

5. Limitations

Data-availability limitations

  • Synthetic, single-center ICU demo. 100-patient date-shifted MIMIC-IV subset; 15 deaths. Estimates are illustrative, not generalizable; calendar time is shifted.
  • Lactate defines the cohort → selection toward severity. Only patients sick enough to have an arterial/venous blood gas are included; the 17 lactate-free patients are excluded, and non-survivors had far more draws (median 15 vs 4), so measurement intensity is itself confounded with outcome.
  • In-hospital death definition. The death table flags 15 deaths; 8 occur during the index (first-lactate) hospitalization and 7 in a later admission (median +531 days). The binary association uses all 15; the KM time axis therefore mixes index-stay and subsequent-encounter mortality — addressed by the 28-day in-hospital landmark analysis, but the full-follow-up log-rank is diluted by the late deaths.
  • SPOKE disease-gene sparsity. Bacterial sepsis (DOID:0040085) has only 1 associated gene; the mechanistic gene programme had to be sourced from the lactic acidosis node (DOID:3650, 188 genes). Sepsis symptom edges were empty. The KG is a documented, knowledge-based context layer, not evidence from this cohort.
  • No standard vocabulary in the demo concept table. Only MIMIC-local concepts ship; linkage relied on measurement_source_concept_id.

Methodological limitations

  • No multivariable adjustment (by design). 15 events cannot support a logistic or Cox model without overfitting; age, sex, and illness severity are unadjusted confounders. The reported association is crude.
  • Underpowered time-to-event inference. Minimum detectable Cohen's d ≈ 0.80 and minimum detectable AUC ≈ 0.73 at 80% power. The non-significant log-rank (p=0.21–0.67) is compatible with a true lactate–mortality effect, not a refutation of it; no "contradiction" is claimed.
  • Follow-up / censoring assumptions. Survivors censored at last hospital discharge; no loss-to-follow-up or competing-risk model. Time zero is the first lactate, so patients contribute follow-up from first measurement, not from a uniform clinical anchor (e.g., ICU admission).
  • Diagnostics not applicable. Standardized mean differences (matching) — not applicable, no matching. Proportional-hazards / Schoenfeld test — not applicable, no Cox model was fitted. These are omitted deliberately, not overlooked.
  • Clearance is noisy. First→last clearance ignores timing and draw count; with a median of 5 results it is an unstable summary and showed no signal.
