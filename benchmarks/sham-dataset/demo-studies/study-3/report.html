Study 3 — Polypharmacy and QT-prolonging drug burden

Original observational study on a synthetic dataset · MedCP (EHR) + SPOKE (knowledge graph) · cross-sectional drug-exposure design

Synthetic data. Analyses use the synthetic, date-shifted MIMIC-IV demo (100 patients, OMOP CDM) served through MedCP. Results are computed from that data, not fabricated. This is an original study on Synthetic Dataset, not a replication of a published paper; every field a replication framework would fill with a source paper is recorded as "original study on Synthetic Dataset".

Provenance legend: EHR measured in the clinical records (via MedCP) · SPOKE derived from the biomedical knowledge graph (via MedCP).

1. Executive summary

Question. Among critically-ill patients on multiple medications, does the burden of QT-prolonging drugs — and the co-prescription of several such drugs in one regimen — track with cardiac-rhythm disturbances recorded in the chart?

Source study. Original study on Synthetic Dataset (no published comparator).

Cohort. EHR Of 100 persons, 77 were exposed to ≥5 distinct named (NDC-resolved) medications and form the polypharmacy cohort. Within the cohort the per-patient named-medication count was a median of 9 (IQR 6–11; range 5–17); across all 100 persons the median was 7 (IQR 5–9). Median age 62 y (IQR 51–72), 42.9% female, in-hospital mortality 16.9% (13/77).

Knowledge-graph exposure. SPOKE The knowledge graph supplied a validated, EHR-independent reference set of 83 compounds that cause the side effect "Electrocardiogram QT prolonged" (SideEffect C0151878). This is knowledge the EHR does not contain.

Headline result — a coverage-driven null. Matching the 83 SPOKE QT-prolongers against the 39 distinct NDC-resolved drug names in the whole dataset yielded 0 matches. The NDC-resolved vocabulary surfaces almost entirely IV fluids, electrolytes and supportive agents (sodium chloride, dextrose, magnesium sulfate, calcium gluconate, pantoprazole, fentanyl, hydromorphone, acetaminophen, vancomycin, ceftriaxone/cefepime, nitroglycerin, phenylephrine, heparin, insulin) — none of which SPOKE flags for QT prolongation. Consequently the measured QT-drug burden was 0 for all 77 cohort patients, the exposure has no variance, and the pre-specified arrhythmia-by-burden test (χ²/trend) is not estimable. The reason is data coverage, not biology: only 2,431 of 18,229 drug rows (13.3%) resolve to a named medication; the other 86.7% carry only raw 11-digit NDC codes (e.g. 63323026201 in 85 patients) with no dictionary entry, and the QT-relevant ICU agents (amiodarone, ondansetron, haloperidol, levofloxacin, sotalol…) live in that unresolved stratum.

Bottom line. Polypharmacy is common and readily measured, and SPOKE contributes a rigorous QT-prolonger reference the EHR lacks; but in this synthetic demo the drug-name coverage is too thin for the resolvable regimen to contain any QT-prolonger, so the QT-burden–arrhythmia association is unanswerable here. Descriptively, tachyarrhythmia annotations are highly prevalent in the cohort (any of AF/sinus-tachycardia/SVT/atrial-flutter: 64/77 = 83.1%, 95% CI 73.2–89.9%), but with a uniformly zero exposure this prevalence cannot be attributed to QT-drug burden.

3. Figures

4. Knowledge-graph contribution (what SPOKE added)

  • SPOKE A validated QT-prolonger reference the EHR does not contain. The clinical record has no field marking a drug as QT-prolonging and (in this demo) no measured QT interval. SPOKE supplied 83 compounds causally linked to "Electrocardiogram QT prolonged" (C0151878) via CAUSES_CcSE — an external pharmacovigilance layer laid over the patients' regimens.
  • SPOKE Interaction modelled as shared side effect. SPOKE has no direct drug–drug-interaction edge, so "clinically relevant interaction" was operationalised as two or more compounds causing the same QT side-effect node in one regimen (additive torsadogenic risk). This is a deliberate, stated proxy for a DDI edge.
  • SPOKE The KG turned an unanswerable question into a diagnosable data gap. By cross-referencing its QT set against the resolvable drug names, the graph revealed that the cohort's measurable medications contain no QT-prolongers, and therefore that the QT-relevant agents must sit in the 86.7% of drug rows that carry only raw NDC codes. The EHR alone could not have surfaced this: it would simply have shown a list of drug names with no notion of QT risk.
Kept separate from EHR facts: every QT flag above is SPOKE-derived knowledge; the medication exposures, NDC coverage, and rhythm annotations are EHR-measured. No QT interval was measured in this dataset — the "QT" label is entirely a knowledge-graph attribute, never a lab value.

5. Limitations

Data-availability limitations

  • NDC name coverage (dominant limitation). Only 2,431/18,229 drug rows (13.3%) resolve to a named medication; 86.7% carry a raw 11-digit NDC code absent from the concept dictionary. Just 39 distinct names exist dataset-wide, dominated by fluids/electrolytes/supportive agents. This — not biology — produces the zero QT-drug burden and the not-estimable primary test.
  • QT flag is knowledge-graph, not measured. There is no QT-interval measurement in this dataset; "QT-prolonging" is entirely a SPOKE attribute. A drug flagged by SPOKE need not have prolonged QT in any given patient, and an unflagged drug is not proven safe.
  • Synthetic, date-shifted, ICU-only demo. 100 patients; dates shifted per-patient (only within-patient intervals meaningful); a critical-care case mix that does not generalise. Small N (and 15 total deaths) precludes multivariable modelling.
  • Rhythm annotations are chart proxies. AF/ST/SVT/atrial-flutter come from nursing "Heart Rhythm" chart labels in condition_occurrence, not adjudicated ECG diagnoses; sinus tachycardia in particular is ubiquitous in the ICU and non-specific.
  • SPOKE completeness. The 83-compound QT set reflects the graph's current knowledge; a truly QT-prolonging drug absent from SPOKE would be missed. (Moot here, since 0 resolvable drugs matched.)

Methodological limitations

  • Exposure invariant → no effect estimate. With burden = 0 for all patients the χ²/trend test and any odds/prevalence ratio are undefined, and the minimum detectable effect at 80% power is undefined (no exposed arm). Larger N cannot fix an ascertainment gap.
  • Cross-sectional, no temporality. Exposure and rhythm annotation are ascertained over the same admission; the design cannot say whether a drug preceded an arrhythmia. Prevalent-user exposure (no washout, no new-user design) further precludes causal reading.
  • Distinct-name counting. The cohort definition counts distinct product-level concept_names, so different bag sizes of one agent count once each and formulation duplicates can inflate counts; the count is a pragmatic exposure-breadth proxy, not an ingredient count.
  • No confounding control. Descriptive/univariate only by design (small N); had burden varied, age, severity and comorbidity would still confound any crude association.
  • Interaction proxy. "Shared QT side effect" approximates additive torsadogenic risk but is not a curated DDI assertion; it cannot capture pharmacokinetic interactions.

6. Reproducibility

All data retrieved through MedCP (harness _harness/medcp_query.py). Clinical SQL → study-F/data/*.csv, SPOKE Cypher → study-F/kg/*.json, statistics in study-F/analysis.py (→ results.json). Full query log in study-F/queries.log. Key queries:

-- Cohort: distinct NDC-resolved medications per person (>=5 = cohort)
SELECT de.person_id, COUNT(DISTINCT c.concept_name) AS n_named_drugs
  FROM drug_exposure de JOIN concept c ON de.drug_source_concept_id=c.concept_id
  WHERE c.vocabulary_id='mimiciv_drug_ndc'
  GROUP BY de.person_id;                         -- 77 persons reach >=5

-- Coverage audit
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM drug_exposure;                                   -- 18,229 rows
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM drug_exposure de JOIN concept c
  ON de.drug_source_concept_id=c.concept_id
  WHERE c.vocabulary_id='mimiciv_drug_ndc';                          -- 2,431 rows (13.3%)

-- Cardiac-rhythm annotations (chart-derived labels)
SELECT DISTINCT person_id, condition_source_value FROM condition_occurrence
  WHERE condition_source_value IN ('AF (Atrial Fibrillation)','ST (Sinus Tachycardia)',
     'SVT (Supra Ventricular Tachycardia)','A Flut (Atrial Flutter)', ...);

-- SPOKE: ALL compounds that CAUSE QT prolongation (fetched once)   [83 compounds]
MATCH (c:Compound)-[:CAUSES_CcSE]->(:SideEffect {identifier:'C0151878'})
  RETURN c.name ORDER BY c.name;
-- Local match of 83 SPOKE names vs 39 NDC-resolved drug names -> 0 matches -> burden=0 for all 77

Appendix — launching prompt (verbatim)

The exact prompt that produced this study:

Run an original observational study, on the synthetic MIMIC-IV/OMOP demo dataset served through MedCP, on polypharmacy and QT-prolonging drug burden in a critical-care cohort. Follow the MedCP clinical study framework skill for the analysis and above all the report, as an original study on a synthetic dataset rather than a replication: wherever the skill expects a source citation, an "Original Paper" result, a replication status, or a paper-versus-replication overlay, record "original study on Synthetic Dataset", and state that the data are synthetic and date-shifted. With MedCP's clinical-record tools, build the cohort of patients exposed to at least five distinct named medications, using the NDC-resolved drug names in drug_exposure (join drug_source_concept_id to the concept dictionary), and describe the distribution of the per-patient medication count. For each patient's regimen, use the SPOKE knowledge graph through MedCP to identify which of their drugs are associated with QT prolongation and which pairs carry clinically relevant interactions — deciding the traversals yourself rather than following a fixed node map — and from this derive a per-patient QT-drug-burden count. Then relate that burden to the cardiac-rhythm annotations in condition_occurrence (for example atrial fibrillation and sinus tachycardia), comparing arrhythmia frequency across burden strata with χ² or a test for trend and reporting counts with a 95% confidence interval. Because many drug records carry only a raw NDC code with no resolvable name, restrict the analysis to NDC-resolved drugs and carry that coverage limitation — together with the fact that the QT flags come from the knowledge graph rather than from a measured QT interval — into the limitations. Attribute every SPOKE-derived flag and statement so the report makes visible exactly which drugs the knowledge graph classified as QT-prolonging or interacting, kept clearly separate from the EHR-measured exposures and rhythms. Include this launching prompt verbatim in the report.
